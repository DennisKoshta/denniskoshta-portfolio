Bells eating counters

The short answer

A bell is a run made of bouncers that keep getting bigger (the wiki's definition). The word is also used for a part of the tape: the striped word (10)^p that the bouncer runs over. The whole run is called a bell because each round is a bouncer over that word, bigger than the last.

In W5-3, each trip of the head adds 1 to a binary counter. When a round ends, the bell eats the counter's lowest digits and grows. To prove W5-3 never halts, we look only at the moments just before each eat, called E(p, N), and show that five rules always give the next one.

10head in B (other states: their colour)time goes down

Left: one bouncer. Each row is one step of W5-3. The first trip is an eat: it runs to the blank left end, cell −5. Every later trip runs left until it meets the defect (one cell that breaks the stripes), changes it, and comes back to the counter on the right.

The turning point moves left with every trip (cell 5, 3, 0, −1, −4, −5), so the zig-zag widens. That widening zig-zag is the bouncer.

Right: many rounds. One row each time the head is back at the counter, so the head (orange) is always at the counter's left end. The stripes are the bell. Each slanted line is the defect walking left: one line, one round.

When the defect is gone, the next trip is an eat: the bell grows at both ends, so its left edge steps left and the head steps right. Rounds mostly get longer; the two short ones near step 3,000 are M4 rounds (section 4).

1Read the tape at an eat

W5-3 at step 165. Left is the bell, 2 pairs of 10 (cells −2 to 1). Then 0 1 1, and the head: B reads the 0 after them (cell 5). Right is the counter: digit 0 is 00, digit 1 is 11, lowest digit first.

The counter reads 0, 0, 1 from the head outward, so N = 100 in binary = 4. We call this tape E(2,4): p = 2 pairs in the bell, counter N = 4. Every eat configuration has the same shape:

E(p, N) = 0^inf (10)^p 0 1 1 B> 0 C(N)

C(N) is the counter: N's binary digits, lowest first, each written twice (00 or 11). B> means "state B reads the next cell to the right".

2One round: count, count, …, count

One row each time the head is back at the counter: B reads the 0 just right of 0 1 1. These moments are the count configurations. Here they go from E(2,4) at step 165 to E(5,7) at step 451, the round in the left picture above. Row 2 is right after the eat (section 3). Rows 3 to 8 are counts, and each one adds 1 to N.

Step through it and watch the bracket marked defect:

  • an extra 1 becomes an extra 0 in the same place (rows 3 → 4 and 5 → 6);
  • an extra 0 becomes an extra 1 two pairs further left (rows 4 → 5 and 6 → 7);
  • at row 8 the defect has walked off the left end. The bell is clean again, (10)^5, and N = 7: that is E(5,7).

Inside two counts, every step (steps 225 to 301)

E runs left over the 10 pairs until it meets the defect, and changes it. Then A runs back right into the counter and adds 1 there (the carry of module 3), and the head ends at B again. The first trip takes 30 steps, the second 46.

3The eat, every step (steps 165 to 197)

With no defect, E's sweep does not stop: it runs off the left end of the bell into the blank. There B and C write 1s, and then C and F sweep right over the whole bell. C writes 1 on each 0 and F writes 0 on each 1, so the stripes slide over by one cell: watch rows 177 to 187. The bell now starts 3 cells further left (cell −5 instead of −2).

Then A runs on into the counter and bites its low digits: 00 00 11 (N = 4) becomes 11 (N = 1), and the head ends 4 cells further right (cell 9 instead of 5). The bell has grown into the eaten cells too and ends with 1 1 1. The next count turns that into a single extra 1: the new defect, at the counter end, ready to walk left.

Why eats are where proofs get hard: W5-3's only undefined transition is F reading 0 (the red cell in the table), and F runs only in this sweep. On a clean bell every cell F reads is a 1, so the machine cannot halt here. The proof has to show that every future eat is like this one.

4Eat to eat: from E(p, N) to E(p′, N′)

Now skip the counts: one row per eat configuration, from the blank tape. The strip shows 14 cells on each side of the head: the end of the bell, 0 1 1, and the counter. Each line of the history starts with its E(p, N). Watch the two numbers.

Which rule comes next depends only on N's lowest binary digits: the digits right of the head. These are rules M1–M5 of W5-3's write-up, in plain words.

N is …next pnext Nseen in the run
odd (M1)p + 2, +1 if p is odd(N + 1)/2 + p + 3E(5,7) → E(8,12)
a multiple of 4 (M2)p + 3, +1 if p is oddN/4 + p + 4E(2,4) → E(5,7)
8m + 2 (M3)p + 4, +1 if p is oddm + p + 6E(30,34) → E(34,40)
16m + 6 (M4)p + 7m + 5E(14,22) → E(21,6)
16k + 14 (M5)p + 7 + tm + 2^t + t + 1, m and t belowE(68,110) → E(76,7)

"+1 if p is odd" means p′ gets one more when p is odd. In M5, write N + 2 = (2m + 1)·2^(t+3) with t ≥ 1. For N = 110: N + 2 = 112 = 7·16, so m = 3, t = 1 and N′ = 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 7. (The write-up also asks for p + 7 ≥ 2^t; that always holds while N ≤ 2p, module 5's invariant.)

Why N stays small. The eat leaves about half or a quarter of N: (N + 1)/2 for odd N, N/4 for N a multiple of 4. Then each count adds 1, and a round has about as many counts as the bell has pairs (drill 2). From E(2,4): the eat leaves 1, then 6 counts give N = 7.

Why M4 and M5 drop N so far. They eat, count once, and eat again. From E(14,22): eat (steps 2,725 → 2,822), one count (→ 2,846), eat (→ 2,953). The two eats bite the four low digits of 22 = 10110 and leave m + 1 = 2. Then two defects cancel each other after just 4 counts (N = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, steps 2,953 → 3,097), instead of one defect walking across the whole bell. So N′ = m + 5 = 6.

W5-4: a counter that counts down

W5-4 1RB0RC_1LC1RB_0RE1LD_0LC1LA_1RF1RA_---0RB. One row per round: the head is back at the wall 00. Each round the bell (q pairs) loses 2 pairs and the counter v loses 1. At 0 the counter refills (here to 6) and grows by one bit, and that round costs 3 pairs. Between rows 7 and 8 the bell is used up: the wall bites the counter, and everything left of it becomes the new bell (q = 15).

The counter's bits sit on every third cell, counted from its right end, and a set bit is a 0 cell. The labels give v, decoded from those cells as in W5-4's write-up. The widget stops at row 14. The next bite is a nested one (the write-up's "Nesting"), and its rows do not follow this pattern.

Recap

  1. Each count adds 1 to the counter and flips the defect; every second count moves it two pairs left. When the defect is gone, the next trip is an eat that shifts the bell and bites the counter.
  2. The eat configurations E(p, N) are the checkpoints: N's lowest binary digits pick one of five rules, which give the next E(p′, N′).
  3. p always grows and N stays small; proving that N ≤ 2p holds forever is the invariant of module 5.
