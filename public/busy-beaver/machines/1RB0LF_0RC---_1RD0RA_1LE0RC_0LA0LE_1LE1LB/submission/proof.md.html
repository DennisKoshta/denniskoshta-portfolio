0. Overview

The tape right of the head is a binary counter written in three tokens, 00, 01 and Z = 100. A digit is b Z 01 Z^g, where b is 00 (bit 0) or 01 (bit 1) and g is the gap to the next digit. The head bounces off the left end, and every bounce adds 1 to the counter (rule Inc, §2).

At the right end of the counter there is a block of gap-1 digits (the R block) followed by a finite-state "tail" W. When the counter overflows, the carry runs into the tail. The tail answers in one of two ways: - S (silent): the counter wraps to 0 and nothing else happens. - N: the whole left part of the tape freezes into a "wall". The machine then spends a long pause eating the wall two increments at a time (§3), rebuilding every gap exactly and possibly inserting new digits.

The proof has four layers: 1. Window lemmas and the parametric counter rules (§2–§3). 2. The overflow (§4): a right sweep to the first 0 bit of the tail, then a leftward return. The return is a 4-rule transducer on the tokens the sweep left behind. On the tail, its result depends only on where the tail's first 0 bit is (14 cases, plus an induction for the tail's growing end unit). Over the R block, its parity decides N or S. 3. TAILFRONT (§4.5): the tail's 2-bit front unit cycles 10 → 01 → 11 → 00 → 10, and S can only happen at 11. 4. Linear arithmetic on the counter parameters (§5): an interval invariant at normal-mode overflows that holds whatever the tail chooses at each 11 slot.

1. Notation and families

1.1 Tape notation

  • 0^inf u X> v 0^inf: state X reads the first cell of v. u <X v: state X reads the last cell of u.
  • Window lemma u <X v → <Y u'v' (or → u'v' Y>): started with the head on the last cell of u (or the first cell of v), the run stays inside the |u|+|v| cells and leaves them on the stated side in state Y. Such a lemma holds in every context.
  • Tokens. Right of the lead, the tape is read as a word over 00, 01 and Z = 100. A right sweep in state C leaves processed tokens behind: 00 and 01 become 10, and Z becomes 010.
  • Inner record (IR): A reads a 0 that it reached moving left, and the cells from the head on read (001)^p 00 0b10001. All macro rules go from one IR to the next. Note that (001)^p 00 = 00 Z^p.
  • Z = 100. A digit is d(b) = 0b10001 = b' Z 01 with b' = 0b. A counter is D_G(v) = d(b_0) Z^(g_1) d(b_1) Z^(g_2) ... d(b_(N-1)), with v = Σ b_i 2^i (LSB leftmost).
  • Zn(g) := (010)^(g+1) is a zone: a gap g frozen into the wall.

1.2 The right counter RC and the tail

  • At every configuration we use, the counter has this form:
  • k ≥ 0 large-gap digits d_0..d_(k-1), with gaps G = (g_1..g_k), every g_i ≥ 7;
  • then the gap-2 digit, followed by the gap Z^2;
  • then the R block U^q, where U = 01100 = 01 Z and q = 2r + H. In digit terms this is r gap-1 digits 0110001100 plus a half digit H ∈ {0,1}.
  • The number of counter digits is N = k + 1 + r + H.
  • The tail is W(f, e) := F(f) (0e_1)(0e_2)...(0e_m) 01, where F(f) = 0x0y0z10001 0?0?10001 0?1000?10001 0?0?1000?10001 0?1000?1000?1000?1000?10001 (11 + 9 + 12 + 14 + 27 = 73 cells: 15 bit cells and 58 fixed cells).
  • f is the 15 bits at the ? places, read left to right; x, y, z are the first three of them, and b4, ..., b15 are the other twelve.
  • The end unit has m ≥ 0 bits e_1..e_m and the end marker 01.
  • The front unit is (y, z).
  • In tokens, every bit is a token 0b, and the fixed cells are tokens Z and 01.
  • L* := the tails with x = 1 and H = y.

1.3 Families

  • K_N(a; D; RC) := 0^inf A>(001)^a 00 D RC (normal mode, lead a).
  • K_P(Wall; p; D; RC) := 0^inf Wall A>(001)^p 00 D RC (pause mode, inner lead p).
  • X(a; G; r, H; f, e) := K_N(a; all bits 1; ...) with the counter of §1.2 and a tail in L*. This is a normal-mode overflow configuration: 0^inf A>(001)^a 00 [0110001 Z^(g_i)]_(i=1..k) 0110001 Z^2 U^(2r+H) W(f, e).
  • The invariant set is the configurations X that satisfy INV (§5.3).

2.2 Rule Inc_L

Inc_L. For every left half-tape L, every a ≥ 0 and every counter D_G(v) with v + 1 < 2^N:

L A>(001)^a 00 D_G(v) T → L <F (001)^a 00 D_G(v+1) T, in 6a + 6S_j + 24j + 7 steps.

Here j = the number of trailing 1 bits of v (b_0..b_(j-1) = 1, b_j = 0) and S_j = g_1 + ... + g_j. The head never reads L before the last step, so L is arbitrary. T is arbitrary too, because the run never reads past d_j.

Proof, in order: 1. R2, R4^a. 2. For i = 0..j-1: R3 on d_i, then R4^(g_(i+1)). 3. R5 on d_j. 4. For i = j-1 down to 0: L1^(g_(i+1)), then L2 on the processed d_i. 5. L1^a, then L3.

The step total is 2 + 3a + Σ(7 + 3g) + 3 + Σ(3g + 17) + 3a + 2. The proof is by induction on the carry depth j, like Climb/E_stmt in CounterClass3v2 TM1. In the .v the counter is ctr gs v (a list of gaps, LSB first, whose last digit is its bit only, so the digits above the carry are never read), the induction is IncC (over the list gs), and IncL adds steps 1 and 5.

2.3 Rule Inc

Inc. K_N(a; D_G(v); T) → K_N(a+1; D_G(v+1); T) for v + 1 < 2^N, in 6a + 6S_j + 24j + 9 steps.

Proof: Inc_L with L = 0^inf, then END. 0^inf 00 <F → 0^inf <A 01 leaves A reading a 0 that it reached moving left, with 0 01 (001)^a 00 = (001)^(a+1) 00 on its right. In the .v: Inc, and on the IR family Inc1, iterated as Incs.

In ZX/ZXN the second Inc_L ends with F on the last cell of a wall that ends ...010 010 100 100 (ZX) or ...0 100 10100100 (ZXN). In both cases the last 12 cells are 010010100100, and ENDZX (22 steps, L4 twice inside it) turns them into 000100011001. A then reads the 13th cell from the end, which is 0, so this is an IR. The domains above are wider than what the run needs: Y and the walls are arbitrary words of the stated shape. In the .v the seven rules are three generic lemmas over an arbitrary left part L, and each wall shape is a choice of L: - TwoInc (Inc_L, B1, Inc_L, END) gives P100, WX, P010 and ZXP1; - ZXg (Inc_L, B1, Inc_L, ENDZX) gives ZX and ZXN; - ZXP2g (Inc_L, BN) gives ZXP2.

On the IR family KP W p gs v H St (wall W, inner lead p, counter ctr gs v, R-block end sfx H, tail St; W = 0^inf is normal mode) they become P100/P100s, WX, P010s, Zone/Zones, ZXN and ZXP (ZXP1 then ZXP2).

4. The overflow

4.1 Right sweep (OvR)

Start from X(a; G; r, H; f, e), or from the second increment of a P-cycle in pause mode at an overflow slot (wall (100)^n 1 with n ≥ 1, which is (100)^(n-1) 1000 after B1). The carry passes every digit:

R2, R4^a, [R3, R4^(g_i)] for i = 1..k, R3 (gap-2 digit), R4^2, then (R6, R4)^q over the R block.

C now stands on the first cell of F(f), and every token to its left is processed: 00→10, 01→10, Z→010. In particular the gap-2 digit is now 1001010 010010 and each of the q units U = 01100 is now P := 10010. In the .v this is OvSweep (R2, then Csweep, since the all-ones counter contains no 00 token).

4.2 The tail's answer: the return transducer

Write T(f) for what the machine does next, as a function of the 15 bits f (in L*, x = 1, so there are 2^14 = 16,384 settings). T(f) is not tabulated case by case. It follows from two facts.

First zero. C keeps going over the tail: R6 passes every 01, R4 passes every Z, and the first 00 token stops it, R7 C> 00 → <E 01. In the tail the 00 tokens are exactly the 0 bits. So C stops at the first 0 bit of f, at a position i ≥ 2 (x = 1), and never reaches the end unit unless f = 1^15 (§4.4). All bits before position i are 1, so the processed word left of the head depends only on i, on the R block and on the left part.

The return transducer. Moving left, the machine now runs a transducer on processed tokens. The head is on the last cell of a processed token, in mode E or F:

mode, processed token(s) left of the head lemma the tokens become new mode
E, 010 L1 010 <E → <E 100 Z E
E, 10 L3 10 <E → <F 00 00 F
F, 10 L3F 10 <F → <E 01 01 E
F, 10 010 with the token 00 on the right UF 10010 <F 00 → <E 00100 01 00 Z, and the 00 on the right becomes 01 E

It stops when no row applies. In the .v this is the function retf on token lists, with soundness lemma retf_spec: whatever retf computes, the machine does. The proof is by induction on a length bound, because the UF row eats two tokens. retf_app composes two runs.

The answer depends only on i. The return starts in mode E over the processed prefix of the tail, so its outcome on the tail is one of 14 fixed computations, one per position of the first 0 (TailRet; the lemmas Tail0z, Tail10 and the 12 cases of Tail11, each closed by computing retf). The table lists them. "Returns in" is the mode in which the head leaves the tail on the left. f' is the 15-bit part after the whole overflow. When the tail returns in F, the cells right of the head are 00 (the token x), and the R-block return of §4.3 (a UF, or BN) turns them into 01, so x' = 1 in every row. The class is N or S as derived in §4.3 (it uses H = y).

4.3 Return through the R block (lemma RB), and the boundary

After OvR and the tail's return (f ≠ 1^15), the head is on the last cell of P^q in state σ (the mode the tail returned in), with the tail's output and the untouched end unit on its right. If σ = F, the cells right of the head are 00. Left of P^q is the processed gap-2 digit 1001010 010010.

Lemma RB. Let σ ∈ {E, F}, q ≥ 0, and let w be any word that starts with 00 if σ = F. Number the units 1..q from the left, and let μ_i be the mode in which unit i is entered: μ_q = σ and μ_i = flip(μ_(i+1)). Let μ := flip(μ_1) = σ flipped q times (μ = σ if q = 0). Then

P^q <σ w → <μ u_1 u_2 ... u_q w*, in Σ_i (5 if μ_i = E, 15 if μ_i = F) steps, where - u_i = t_i 100, with t_1 = 00, and for i ≥ 2, t_i = 01 if μ_(i−1) = F and 00 if μ_(i−1) = E; - w = w with its first token replaced by 01 if q ≥ 1 and σ = F, and w = w otherwise.

Proof, by induction on q. For q = 0 there is nothing to do. For q + 1, the first lemma applied is to the rightmost unit: - σ = E: UE 10010 <E → <F 00100 leaves P^q <F 00100 w. The new right word 00100 w starts with 00, so the induction hypothesis applies with mode F. It gives units 1..q, turns the first token of 00100 into 01 if q ≥ 1 (so t_(q+1) = 01 exactly when μ_q = F, as claimed), and leaves w unchanged (w = w, as claimed for σ = E). - σ = F: w = 00 w' by assumption. UF 10010 <F 00 → <E 00100 01 leaves P^q <E 00100 01 w'. The hypothesis applies with mode E, which has no precondition. It gives units 1..q and does not touch its right word, so u_(q+1) = 00100 (t_(q+1) = 00, matching μ_q = E) and w = 01 w' (matching σ = F).

In both cases the mode flips once more, so the final mode is σ flipped q + 1 times. ∎

So the 00-on-the-right precondition of every UF is carried by the induction: inside the block it is the 00100 just written by UE, and at the right end it is the 00 that a tail returning in F leaves in front of itself (§4.2). In the .v, lemma RB is four closed forms of the transducer, each by induction on the number of unit pairs: RB_E2 and RB_F2 (an even number of units, entered in E or F) and RB_E1 and RB_F1 (an odd number).

The boundary. μ = F gives N and μ = E gives S. - μ = F → N. The two cells right of the head are 00: the token t_1 = 00 of u_1 if q ≥ 1, or the first token of w if q = 0 (then σ = μ = F, and the tail left 00). BN 010010 <F 00 → <A 00010001 rewrites the processed Z Z and that token. A then reads the last cell of the processed gap-2 digit 1001010, which is 0, so this is an IR with lead 0. Reading the cells from A: 0 000100 01 ... = 00 | 00100 | 01 .... - BN therefore re-tokenises the processed gap-2 digit. Its first 6 cells 100101 join the wall, its 7th cell and the first cell of BN's output form the lead 00, and the next 5 cells form a new unit u_0 = 00100. - BN also sets t_1 to 01, or the first token of w when q = 0. This is why the R block grows leftward. - From normal mode this is rule NO: X(a; G; r, H; f, e) → 0^inf (100)^a 101001 [Zn(g_i) 1001]_(i=1..k) 01 A> 00 RC'. This is just the processed left part read as a wall: (100)^a 10 [1001010 (010)^(g_i)]_i 100101 (wallOv in the .v). - From pause mode (n ≥ 1) it is rule PO: 0^inf (100)^n 1 A>(001)^p 00 D(2^N-2) RC → 0^inf (100)^n Zn(p) 1001 [Zn(g_i) 1001]_i 01 A> 00 RC'. This comes from Inc_L(j = 0), B1, then OvR with left context (100)^(n-1) 1000 (wallPO). - μ = E → S. The transducer goes on. Over the processed gap-2 digit it is BS 1001010010010 <E → <E 0010001100100, which restores that digit with bit 0. Over each processed large digit it is [L1^(g_i), L2], for i = k..1, and over the lead it is L1^a and then L3, as in Inc_L. Each digit is restored with bit 0, and F stops on the cell left of the lead (retf_dig, retf_LD, retf_lead). - From normal mode, END gives rule SILENT: X(...) → K_N(a+1; D_G(0); RC') with the gap-2 digit kept. The counter wraps and there is no pause. - From pause mode, END gives rule SILENTP: (100)^n 1 A>(001)^p 00 D(2^N-2) → (100)^(n-1) 1 A>(001)^(p+1) 00 D(0), for n ≥ 1.

The new right counter RC' (the word after the lead 00, minus the gap-2 digit, which is rebuilt by the first ZX after N and kept after S). Write q = 2r + H. From RB, μ_i alternates. - N (μ = F, so μ_1 = E): RC' = u_0 u_1 ... u_q w. The first tokens are 00 at even indices and 01 at odd indices (u_0 = 00100, t_1 set to 01 by BN, and for i ≥ 2, t_i = 01 iff μ_(i−1) = F iff i is odd). The first token of w is 01 if q is even (σ = μ_q = F, and the UF on u_q, or BN when q = 0, set it) and is the tail's own first token if q is odd (σ = E, and §4.2's table keeps x = 1). Either way the tail now starts with 01, so x' = 1. - The pairs (u_0 u_1), (u_2 u_3), ... are 00100 01100 = 0010001 100 = d(0) Z, R digits with bit 0 at gap 1. - q odd (H = 1): q + 1 units pair up, so r' = (q+1)/2 = r + 1 and H' = 0, and the tail follows. - q even (H = 0): u_q = 00100 and the 01 after it form the half digit 0010001, so r' = q/2 = r and H' = 1. That 01 is also x' = 1. - Either way 2r' + H' = q + 1, H' = 1 − H, and the emission is e = (r'+H') − (r+H) = 1 − H. - S (μ = E, so μ_1 = F): RC' = u_1 ... u_q w, with first tokens 00 at odd i and 01 at even i. - By the class rule below, S needs y = H = 1. So q = 2r + 1 is odd, σ = μ_q = F, and the UF on u_q has set the first token of w to 01. So x' = 1 again. - The pairs (u_1 u_2), ..., (u_(q−2) u_(q−1)) are d(0) Z. - u_q and the 01 after it form the half digit, and that 01 is x' = 1. - So r' = r, H' = 1 = H, and e = 0. - In both cases RC' is U^(2r'+H') W(f', e) except that every R digit has bit 0 (its bit token is 00, not 01). By the next overflow the counter has counted these bits up to 1 again, which is the form X needs. The end unit was never read, so e and m are unchanged.

Class rule. N ⇔ μ = F ⇔ σ flipped 2r + y times is F. For y = 0 the tail returns in F (§4.2, first 0 at y), so the class is N. For y = 1, N ⇔ σ = E. This is the class column of §4.2's table, and it does not depend on r.

L* closure and the front unit. - Class N: x' = 1 and y' = 1 − y = H' (§4.2's table). - Class S: x' = 1 and y' = y = H = H'.

So the new tail is in L*, and (y, z) → (y', z') is the TAILFRONT arrow, with emission e = 1 − y for N and e = 0 for S. All of this holds for every r ≥ 0, every a and G, and both normal and pause context, because the tail's return never reads left of the tail, and RB, BN and the S return are parametric. In the .v the three overflow outcomes are OvN1 (H = 1, the tail returns in E, N, H' = 0), OvN0 (H = 0, the tail returns in F, N, H' = 1 and one more R digit) and OvS1 (H = 1, the tail returns in F, S), each for an arbitrary left context.

4.4 ENDCARRY (f = 1^15)

After CF1, C reads the end unit (0e_1)...(0e_m) 01. Let j = the number of leading 1s of e, with j := m + 1 when all m bits are 1. In that case the end marker 01 counts as one more 1, the first blank 00 after it is the first 0, R7 writes a new marker there, and m grows by 1.

R6^j and R7 run, and then the transducer of §4.2 returns over the j processed 10 tokens: alternately L3 (E→F, gives 00) and L3F (F→E, gives 01), the rightmost first, in mode E. So token i of the end unit (1 ≤ i ≤ j) becomes 00 if j − i is even and 01 if j − i is odd, and the mode on the last cell of P1 is E for even j and F for odd j (retf_PP, retf_PPP). - j even: PEN P1 <E → <E F(100100010001010). Then lemma RB with σ = E and w = F(100100010001010)·(end unit), q = 2r + 1 (H = y = 1), so μ = F and the result is N. The end bits become (10)^(j/2) 1 rest, f' = 100100010001010, H' = 0, e0. - j odd: PFN P1 <F → <F F(010000100100101), which never reads the end unit, so its first token stays 00 (e'_1 = 0). Then RB with σ = F: w = F(010000100100101)·(end unit) starts with 00, as RB needs. q = 2r + 1 is odd, so μ = E and the result is S. The UF on u_q sets the first token to 01, so f' = 110000100100101 and H' = H = 1, e0. The end bits become (01)^((j+1)/2) rest. - Both f' give the TAILFRONT arrows from front 11: 11 →N,e0→ 00 and 11 →S→ 10. So ENDCARRY also closes L* and follows TAILFRONT.

In the .v this is Tail11_end: e_split gives e = 1^j 0 e' or e = 1^m, and the parity of j picks the case. PEN and PFN are the two fixed transducer runs F1E_ret and F1F_ret.

4.5 TAILFRONT and closure of L*

For every tail in L* and every r, a, G, in normal or pause context, the overflow is N or S with exactly the forms above. The new tail is again in L*, with m' = m except in the ENDCARRY growth case. The front unit and the emission follow:

TAILFRONT:   10 -N,e0-> 01 -N,e1-> 11 -N,e0-> 00 -N,e1-> 10,     11 -S,e0-> 10

S occurs only from front unit 11. - Proof: §4.2 (the first-zero cases), lemma RB with the boundary analysis (§4.3), and ENDCARRY (§4.4). Every statement over r, a, G and the context is parametric. The only computations are 16 fixed transducer runs: 14 first-zero cases, plus PEN and PFN.

5. Macro dynamics and the invariant

5.1 Quantities at a configuration X(a; G; r, H; f, e)

  • R := r + H, N = k + 1 + R, M = 2^N;
  • Γ := Σ_(i=1..k) (g_i − 1)·2^(i−1);
  • c := 2^(k+R) − 2^(k+1) − 2Γ − 4;
  • s := a − c, κ := c − M/4.

5.2 One overflow and its pause, in closed form

Slot rule. In a pause the wall is (100)^w 1 and each P100 cycle lowers w by 1 and adds 2 to v. Let the next overflow slot be cyc = (2^N − 2 − v)/2 cycles away. - If cyc ≤ w − 1, the slot is reached with wall (100)^(w−cyc) 1, where w − cyc ≥ 1, and value 2^N − 2. This is a PO slot: PO, or SILENTP, both need n ≥ 1. - If cyc ≥ w + 1, after w cycles the wall is the bare 1 and v + 2 < 2^N, so WX ends the pause. - If cyc = w, the slot would be reached with the bare wall 1. No rule covers this: PO and SILENTP need n ≥ 1, and WX needs v + 2 < 2^N. INV keeps every path away from it (§5.3).

After NO with emission e: R' = R + e and M' = 2^e·M. - The first ZX (p = 0) creates the gap-2 digit. The zones are then rebuilt innermost first: for each zone, (g − 2) P010 cycles, then ZX (ZXN for the last one). Every gap is restored exactly. - After ZXN the counter value is v_0 = 2^(k+2) + 4Γ, the wall is (100)^(a−2) 1 and the lead is 0. - The pause then runs P100 cycles (+2 each). The next overflow slot comes after c' = (M' − 2 − v_0)/2 cycles; for e = 0 this is c' = c + 3. - By the slot rule with w = a − 2: if c' ≤ a − 3, the slot is a PO slot with p = c' and wall a − 2 − c' ≥ 1. If c' ≥ a − 1, WX ends the pause and Inc runs to the next normal-mode overflow. c' = a − 2 never occurs under INV.

After PO at inner lead p with emission e: the gap-2 digit and the zones of G are rebuilt as above. Then the zone Zn(p) is rebuilt: (p − 2) P010 cycles, then ZXP1 and ZXP2, which inserts the new gap p − 1. The wall drops by 3 (ZXP1 by 1, ZXP2 by 2). The value is 2(v_G + 2(p−2) + 2) + 2.

The four paths between consecutive normal-mode overflows, starting from front unit f. Each is a composition of the rules above (algebra below, all in terms of M and s):

path from f outcomes M' s' κ' f'
S 11 S M s + M κ 10
P1 10 or 11 NO e0, PO e1, WX 4M M + 10 − s 2 11 or 10
P1S 10 NO e0, PO e1, SILENTP, WX 4M 5M + 10 − s 2 10
P3 10 NO e0, PO e1, PO e0, PO e1, WX 32M 9M + 32 − s 8 10

The algebra: - S: SILENT gives lead a + 1 and counter 0. Then M − 1 Incs reach the next overflow at a + M, with G and R unchanged. - NO e0: p = c + 3, so PO#1 has wall s − 5 and inner lead p = c + 3. - PO#1 (e1): G'' = (c+2) ∪ G, R'' = R + 1, M'' = 4M. Using 2p = M − 2^(k+2) − 4Γ − 2, the value after ZXP2 is v_1 = 2M − 6. The wall is s − 8 and the lead 0. - The next slot comes after (4M − 2 − v_1)/2 = M + 2 cycles. - If M + 2 ≥ (s − 8) + 1, i.e. s ≤ M + 9: WX gives lead s − 7 and value v_1 + 2(s − 7). The next overflow is at a'' = 2M + 12 − s. With the new gaps, c'' = M + 2 (so κ'' = 2) and s'' = M + 10 − s. This is P1. - If M + 2 ≤ (s − 8) − 1, i.e. s ≥ M + 11: PO#2 is reached at p = M + 2 with wall s − M − 10 ≥ 1. Its front unit is 11. - s = M + 10 is the excluded case cyc = w. INV gives s ≤ M − 14 or s ≥ M + 24, so it never occurs. - S at PO#2 (SILENTP): p = M + 3, wall s − M − 11, value 0. The next slot is 2M − 1 ≥ (s − M − 11) + 1 cycles away (s ≤ 3M + 9), so WX gives lead s − 7 and value 2(s − M − 10). Then a'' = 6M + 12 − s and s'' = 5M + 10 − s. This is P1S. - e0 at PO#2: gap M + 1 is inserted, M''' = 8M, value 8M − 18, wall s − M − 13. The next slot is 8 ≤ (s − M − 13) − 1 cycles away (s ≥ M + 22): PO#3 at p = 8 with wall s − M − 21. Its front unit is 00, so the outcome is e1. - PO#3 (e1): gap 7 is inserted, M4 = 32M, value 16M − 18, wall s − M − 24 ≥ 0. The next slot is 8M + 8 ≥ (s − M − 24) + 1 cycles away, so WX follows. Then a'' = 17M + 40 − s, c'' = 8M + 8 (κ = 8) and s'' = 9M + 32 − s. This is P3.

5.3 The invariant

INV at a configuration X: M ≥ 256 (N ≥ 8), κ ∈ {2, 8}, all gaps ≥ 7, tail in L*, and

  • f = 11 and 24 ≤ s ≤ M/4 + 2, or
  • f = 10 and s ∈ [8, M − 14] ∪ [M + 24, 2M].

Notes on INV: - M ≥ 256 is preserved: M' ∈ {M, 4M, 32M} on every path, and the initial configuration has M = 256. - It is needed: the side conditions below use M ≥ 256 and c = M/4 + κ ≥ 66. Without it, INV as written would also admit N = 5 (k = 0, R = 4, κ = 2) and N = 7 configurations, where the argument does not apply. - It gives k ≥ 1. From c = M/4 + κ, Γ = 2^(N−3) − 2^k − 2 − κ/2. k = 0 needs Γ = 0, which means N = 5. So under INV there is at least one large-gap digit, and the last zone of every NO rebuild is a ZXN preceded by a ZX.

Which path runs (front unit and s decide; at a 11 slot both choices are covered):

front unit f s paths why
11 [24, M/4+2] S or P1 (the tail chooses) 11 allows S or e0; after e0 the front unit is 00, which forces e1 at PO#1; then s ≤ M + 9, so WX
10 [8, M−14] P1 10 forces e0, then 01 forces e1 at PO#1; s ≤ M + 9, so WX
10 [M+24, 2M] P1S or P3 after PO#1 the front unit is 11; s ≥ M + 11, so the PO#2 slot is reached; the tail chooses S (P1S) or e0 (P3)

Closure (images of the interval end points; every map has slope ±1):

path image of s target
S [M+24, 5M/4+2] inside [M+24, 2M], f = 10
P1 from 11 [3M/4+8, M−14] inside [8, 4M−14] = [8, M'−14], f = 10
P1 from 10 [24, M+2] exactly [24, M'/4+2], f = 11
P1S [3M+10, 4M−14] inside [8, M'−14], f = 10
P3 [7M+32, 8M+8] inside [8, M'−14] with M' = 32M, f = 10

Side conditions that keep every rule inside its domain. They are linear in s, so the interval end points suffice. Below, M ≥ 256 and c = M/4 + κ ≥ 66: - NO: a = s + c ≥ 74 ≥ 2 (ZXN needs n ≥ 2). - PO#1 slot: w = a − 2 and cyc = c + 3, so cyc ≤ w − 1 ⇔ s ≥ 6. PO#1 has p = c + 3 ≥ 69 and wall s − 5 ≥ 3 (ZXP1 n ≥ 3, ZXP2 n ≥ 2). - After PO#1: w = s − 8 and cyc = M + 2. WX for f = 11 and for f = 10 with s ≤ M − 14, since s ≤ M + 9. PO#2 for s ≥ M + 24, since s ≥ M + 11. - PO#2: p = M + 2 and wall s − M − 10 ≥ 14. - SILENTP at PO#2: wall s − M − 10 ≥ 14 ≥ 1. Afterwards WX, since 2M − 1 ≥ s − M − 10. - PO#3 slot: w = s − M − 13 and cyc = 8 ≤ w − 1. PO#3 has p = 8 and wall s − M − 21 ≥ 3. Afterwards WX, since 8M + 8 ≥ s − M − 23. - New gaps: c + 2 ≥ 68, M + 1 and 7 are all ≥ 7. So every ZX zone has g + 1 ≥ 8 ≥ 3 blocks, and every PO has p ≥ 8, so its zone Zn(p) is rebuilt by p − 2 ≥ 6 P010 cycles, then ZXP1 and ZXP2. - The rebuild never overflows. Let the slack be (2^N − 2 − v)/2, the number of +2 cycles left before the next slot. - A zone of gap g (g − 2 P010 cycles, then a ZX or ZXN that inserts gap g; the first ZX at lead 0 is the case g = 2) needs slack ≥ g − 1 before it. It leaves slack' = 2(slack − g + 2) − 1, since the ZX adds 2 and then doubles the value with one more digit. - The PO zone Zn(p) (p − 2 P010 cycles, ZXP1, then ZXP2, which adds 1 and doubles) needs slack ≥ p before it and leaves slack' = 2(slack − p + 1). - Inverting: slack before = (slack' + 2g − 3)/2 ≥ g − 1 ⇔ slack' ≥ 1, and slack before = slack'/2 + p − 1 ≥ p ⇔ slack' ≥ 2. - At the end of every rebuild the slack is the distance to the next slot: c + 3, M + 2, 8 or 8M + 8, all ≥ 8. Going backwards, every zone had enough slack. So no carry reaches the tail during a rebuild. - S inside a pause happens only at PO#2. PO#1 and PO#3 have front unit 01 or 00. After SILENTP the pause ends with WX before another slot (above).

In the .v. INV a G r1 St is this invariant at the configuration KX a G r1 St. At a normal-mode overflow the front unit is 11 or 10, so H = y = 1, and the R block is r1 = r gap-1 digits plus the half digit. With Q := 2^(|G| + r1) = M/4 and gval G := Σ_i 2^i g_i, INV says: - all gaps are ≥ 2 (this is all the rules need; the run has gaps ≥ 7), and Q ≥ 64; - κ ∈ {2, 8}, with gval G + 6 + κ = Q (this is c = M/4 + κ), and a = s + Q + κ; - the tail St is in L* with front 11 and 24 ≤ s ≤ Q + 2, or with front 10 and s ∈ [8, 4Q − 14] ∪ [4Q + 24, 8Q].

The paths are pathS (a ≥ 1), pathP1 (s ∈ [8, M + 9]), pathP1S (s ∈ [M + 11, 3M + 9]) and pathP3 (s ∈ [M + 24, 9M + 31]). pre1 is the part shared by the last three (NO e0, the pause, PO#1 e1). Each path takes the tail's behaviour at each overflow as a hypothesis (OvNp, OvSp), and step supplies it from §4.2's lemmas. The rebuild's no-overflow condition is stated only on the final value (Zones); zv_ge (2^|L|·v ≤ zv L v) carries it back to every earlier zone. This is the slack argument above in one line. step checks the image intervals with lia.

7. Conclusion

  • Coverage. Every member of the invariant set takes one of the paths S, P1, P1S, P3, whatever the tail chooses at each 11 slot. Each path is a finite composition of rules (Inc, SILENT, NO, PO, SILENTP, P100, WX, P010, ZX, ZXN, ZXP1, ZXP2), each applied inside its domain (§5.3). Each path lands in the invariant set again (step).
  • Progress. Each path contains an overflow, and every overflow starts with R2, so each path takes at least one step (-->+). progress_nonhalt_cond with P := INV, after multistep_nonhalt with init, gives nonhalt.
  • Halting. Nothing more is needed: every rule is a derivation in busycoq's step relation, and a halting configuration has no successor. (Informally: the only halting transition is B reading 1, B is entered only from A0 1RB and F1 1LB, and in every window run above, the B that follows reads 0.)

8. Mapping to busycoq

The template, TM1

CounterClass3v2.v, Module TM1 proves a multi-level counter by hand: sweep lemmas, an induction on the carry depth (E_stmt, E_all), Climb/Climbs, macro lemmas Mac_*, then a trajectory family Traj n proved by strong induction, and nonhalt by progress_nonhalt_simple. - Kept: window lemmas by es, shift rules by shift_rule_R/shift_rule_L, a carry lemma by induction on the carry depth (IncC, as E_stmt/E_all), macro lemmas chained with follow, and multistep_nonhalt from an exact initial segment. - Changed: the counter is a list of gaps read in tokens (ctr gs v), not TM1's unary blocks. The closing argument is not a strong induction over a trajectory family. It is an invariant on macro states (a, G, r1, tail) with progress_nonhalt_cond, because the tail's choices at front 11 make the orbit non-uniform. - New: the token layer; the return transducer retf with retf_spec, which handles the overflow, the R block and the tail without a 16,384-entry table; one IR shape KP for normal and pause mode, so each pause rule is one generic lemma; the tail's behaviour as a hypothesis of the path lemmas (OvNp, OvSp); and the zone-rebuild induction Zones with its side condition on the final value only.
