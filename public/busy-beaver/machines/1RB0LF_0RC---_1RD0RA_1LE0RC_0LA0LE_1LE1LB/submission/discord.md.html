`1RB0LF_0RC---_1RD0RA_1LE0RC_0LA0LE_1LE1LB` (Sync bouncer counter, sheet row 918) never halts. Coq proof: <LINK>

It's a spread binary counter inside a bouncer. Right of the lead `(001)^a 00` the tape is digits `b 100 01 (100)^g` at unary gaps, LSB first, and each bounce adds 1 to the counter and to a. On overflow the carry runs through a block of gap-1 digits into a finite tail that answers N or S. On S the counter just wraps. On N the left part freezes into a wall of zones that a long pause eats two +1s at a time, rebuilding every gap and inserting new digits (the 28, 68, 260, 1028, 4097, 7 gaps in the run). The tail is itself a small mixed-radix counter: its front unit cycles 10 -> 01 -> 11 -> 00 -> 10, and S can only happen at 11. In Coq the leftward overflow return is a 4-rule token transducer proved sound once, so the tail's answer depends only on where its first 0 bit is (14 cases by computation + an induction for the growing end unit), with no 2^14-entry table. Between normal-mode overflows only 4 paths occur, and an interval invariant on (2^N, a - c, front unit) is closed under all of them, whichever way the tail goes at 11. Init is 92,299 steps.

One file, top-level `Lemma nonhalt : ~ halts tm c0.`, Coq 8.20.1, busycoq 0940bb9c, imports only Individual62. Compiles in ~10 s (native), `Print Assumptions` closed, coqchk passes. Architecture adapted from CounterClass3v2 TM1.
