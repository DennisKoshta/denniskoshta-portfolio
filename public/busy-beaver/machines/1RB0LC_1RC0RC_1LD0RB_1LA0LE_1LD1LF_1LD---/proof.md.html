1. Notation and two exact facts

1.1 K-configurations

K(L; a, b) := 0^inf L (10)^a 0^3 (10)^b B> 0^inf          L an arbitrary finite word (the "left text")
digit d = (u, beta, v):   (u,0,v) := (10)^u 00 (01)^v      (u,1,v) := (10)^u (01)^v
K(d_1 .. d_m; a, b) := K(10 01 d_1 .. d_m; a, b)           as in the parent
  • a and the (10) run before 0^3 are maximal, so L never ends in 10.
  • The parent's family has L = 10 01 d_1 .. d_m. In the twin the left text leaves that shape at step 428 (§4). After that it is a word that ends in digits but starts with irregular text, sometimes at odd parity.
  • Window-lemma notation is the parent's: X> means state X on the next cell, <X means state X on the previous cell. A window lemma runs inside its window, so it holds in every tape context.

1.2 Table facts (check 1)

  • The only halting transition is F reading 1.
  • F is entered only by E1 (1LF).
  • In the twin, E is entered only by D1 (0LE). In the parent, A1 also enters E. This is the one change.
  • So a halt needs a leftward run D, E, F reading 1, 1, 1: the pattern 111 <D.

1.3 Token machine (check 2)

Pair the cells in the head's parity: the head stands between tokens z = 00, o = 01, a = 10 and X = 11. Every visit of the head to a token starts and ends on a token boundary. So the machine is exactly the following 4-symbol machine, with 5 left-moving states and 2 right-moving states (C' is C entered from the left). Each entry lists the new token, the exit state and the direction. All 28 entries are checked against the cell machine.

left-moving (enters the token on its left)
E:  a->o E   z->X A   o->X D   X->HALT
D:  o->a D   X->a F   a->o C   z->a C' (turns right)
F:  a->o E   z->X A   o->HALT  X->HALT
A:  o->a D   X->o C'  z->o B   a->X B      (the last three turn right)
C:  a->o E   z->X A   o->z B   X->a B      (the last two turn right)
right-moving (enters the token on its right)
B:  o->a B   X->z B   z->o E   a->X A      (the last two turn left)
C': a->o C'  X->z C'  z->a D   o->X D      (the last two turn left)

The blank start is the entry (A, z): cell 0 is the right cell of its token. So the token machine describes the whole run. Halting therefore needs E to meet an X, or F to meet an o or an X, and F comes only from D meeting an X. "Misaligned" left text is not a special case for the machine. It only breaks the digit description. §3.6 states the same hazards in cell terms, at the one place where they can occur.

3.5 The general rule G (checks 8, 11)

Let K(L; a, b) with a ≥ 1.

Swept suffix. Read L from the right and peel off maximal digits (10)^u (01)^v (u, v ≥ 1, bit 1): - a digit with u ≥ 2 is swept by Lb, and peeling continues; - a digit with u = 1 and a 0 on its left (a blank counts as 0) is swept by Lb1, and peeling stops, because the cell on its left is 0 and not the end of a digit; - a digit with u = 1 and a 1 on its left is the bounce digit. It is not swept, and the turn is Lt.

What remains is the text X, so L = X d_p .. d_m with d_p .. d_m the swept digits (k = m − p + 1 ≥ 0). X does not end in 10 and does not end in a digit, by maximality.

Phases 1–2. As in M (§3.1). Phase 1 reads only the a/b block. Phase 2 sweeps the a block and then Lb or Lb1 on d_m, .., d_p. The head is then at X <E, or at X <C before a 0 after Lb1, which acts the same (§2).

Turn. On the last cells of X (or on the bounce digit), with X' never read:

turn the text becomes steps exits made of
Lf X' 00 (01)^v, v ≥ 1 X' 10 (01)^(v−1) C> 11 4v+1 C T4 S3^(v−1) T6 S2^(v−1)
T3S2 X' 1100 X' 01 C> 11 5 C T3 S2
Jc X' 00 (01)^v 00, v ≥ 1 X' 10 (01)^v C> 11 4v+5 C T3j S3^(v−1) T6 S2^(v−1) S2
T2 X' 000 X' 100 B> 5 B T2 (T2c after Lb1)
Lt X' 1 10 (01)^v (bounce digit) X' 01 (10)^(v−1) 000 B> 8v+1 B T4 S3^(v−1) T5b S6^(v−1) T10

Return and right end. - M* (C exit). Rb on d_p, .., d_m, then M's phase 3 tail (S5^a, T8, S1^(b+2)): K(X'·t, d_p..d_m; a, b) --> K(X'·w, (u_i,0,v_i−1)_{i=p..m}; a+1, b+2) steps 31 + 8a + 8b + c + Σ_i (8u_i + 4v_i) c = 4v+1 (Lf), 5 (T3S2), 4v+5 (Jc) (t, w) = (00 (01)^v, 10 (01)^(v−1)) for Lf, (1100, 01) for T3S2, (00 (01)^v 00, 10 (01)^v) for Jc Lf with X' = X'' (10)^u is M: the digit (u,0,v) becomes (u+1,1,v−1). T3S2 turns .. 1 1 00 into .. 0 1. Jc removes a junction (0,0,0) after a bit-0 digit (u,0,v) and flips that digit to (u+1,1,v) without spending a 01. - O* (B exit). Ro on d_p, .., d_m, then O's phases 5–7 (§3.2). Phase 6 sweeps left over (10)^(a−1) and turns (T2) in the 000 that the last Ro left, or that Lt left when there is no swept digit: T2: K(X' 000, d_p..d_m; a, b) --> K(X', (1,1,u_p), (v_p−1,0,u_{p+1}), .., (v_{m−1}−1,0,u_m), (v_m,1,a); b+3, 1) steps 81 − 4k + 8a + 20b + 4 Σ_{i>=p} u_i + 8 Σ_{i>=p} v_i, needs k >= 1 Lt: the P rule of §3.3 (k counts the bounce digit there) On the tape, the T2 result is X' 10 (01)^u_p (10)^(v_p−1) 00 (01)^u_{p+1} .. (10)^v_m (01)^a (10)^(b+3) 000 10 B>. T2 needs k ≥ 1. With k = 0 the turn leaves 100 where phase 6 expects 000 (class U0 below).

Special cases. - M is Lf with every swept digit u ≥ 2. - O is T2 with d_p = the wall digit (1,1,1) (swept by Lb1) and X' = 0^inf. The formula then gives O's output and O's step count. 3.6 Case analysis of the sweep (check 12)

The left-moving part of the table is a deterministic automaton. It reads one cell, writes, and moves one cell left; R means it turns right into B:

E: 0 -> 1, D    1 -> 1, F          D: 0 -> 1, A    1 -> 0, E          F: 0 -> 1, D    1 -> HALT
A: 0 -> 1, R    1 -> 0, C          C: 0 -> 1, D    1 -> 0, R          (C0 = E0)

Read right to left, (01)^v takes E to D (T4, S3), and (10)^u takes D to C (u = 1, T5) or to E (u ≥ 2, T5 S4c S4). From C, a 0 continues as from E, and a 1 is the bounce (T5b, i.e. Lt). So after the swept suffix the head is in E at the end of X, or in C before a 0. Since X ends neither in 10 nor in a digit, its ending falls in exactly one class:

class X ends in automaton path, right to left outcome
Lf 00 (01)^v E1 F0 (D1 E0)^(v−1) D0 A0 R G (M*)
T2 000 E0 D0 A0 R G (O*) if k ≥ 1
T3S2 1100 E0 D0 A1 C1 R G (M*)
Jc 00 (01)^w 0100 (= 00 (01)^(w+1) 00) E0 D0 A1 C0 (D1 E0)^w D0 A0 R G (M*)
H1 11 E1 F1 halts
H2 111 (01)^v E1 F0 (D1 E0)^(v−1) D1 E1 F1 halts
U1 011 (01)^v .. D1 E1 F0, then D goes on left not analysed
U2 10 (01)^w 0100 E0 D0 A1 C0 (D1 E0)^w D0 A1, then C goes on not analysed (C bounces on a 1, goes on over a 0)
U3 11 (01)^w 0100 E0 D0 A1 C0 (D1 E0)^w D1 E1, then F halts if the next cell is 1, else not analysed
U0 000 with k = 0 T2, then phase 6 meets 100 not analysed

(Lt is decided before X, in the swept suffix. The bounce digit is never part of X.)

Statement (relative to an invariant). For every K-configuration K(L; a, b) with a ≥ 1, exactly one of the following holds: - G applies, and its macro step, a composition of non-halting windows, ends in a K-configuration with a' = a+1 or a' = b+3, both ≥ 1; - the macro step halts (H1, H2); - X is in U0–U3.

So the run never halts if every reachable K-configuration is in one of the covered classes (Lf, T2 with k ≥ 1, T3S2, Jc, or Lt). That is the open §5.

Check 12 confirms three things: - the automaton against the table; - the partition on all 21,852 texts X of length ≤ 14 that end neither in 10 nor in a digit (H1 8,191, H2 1,359, Jc 694, Lf 2,744, T2 4,098, T3S2 2,047, U1 1,365, U2 677, U3 677); - the behaviour on 4,300 K-configs built from them: G is exact on Lf/T2/T3S2/Jc/Lt, and H1/H2 halt inside the macro step.

In token terms, H1 is the review's "E meets an X token". H2, U1 and U3 contain its "D meets an X, then F" step. F halts when it then reads a 1: always in H2, and in U3 when the next cell is 1. In the run to 10^10, no K-configuration is in H or U (check 8, and the 10^10 scratch run).

5. Invariant: OPEN

What a proof still needs (sharper after §3.5–§3.6). A family F of K-configurations such that: 1. F contains the step-609 configuration (or a later one); 2. no member of F has a = 0 or a left text in H1, H2 or U0–U3; 3. G maps F into F.

Then, by induction over macro steps and §3.6, the machine never halts.

Why this is hard: - Shape alone fails (check 10). K((1,0,2); 2, 1) has a plain wall, a ≥ 1 and b odd, and it halts at step 2535. G predicts all 13 of its macro steps. Its last K-config (step 2384) is 1001 1101 0101 0010 1010 1 + (10)^19 000 (10)^13. Its swept suffix ends at X = 10011, which is H1: an all-ones sweep passes the u = 4 digit and E reads 11. In the box m ≤ 2, u, v ∈ 1..3, beta ∈ {0,1}, a, b ∈ 0..3, 807 of 5,472 canonical K-configs halt within 2·10^6 steps. So F has to carry quantitative constraints, as the parent's parities and 2^j bounds did. - X tokens are present in the left text. 5,259 of 22,436 K-configs to 10^9 have an X token in their left text, always with z or o to its right and never an a-run (review count). The parent's class-A overflow (top (1,1,v)) is always a P in the twin. Its observation shows nested "fossil-K" walls whose fuel drops by exactly 1 per level-1 overflow, and walls that dissolve and re-form when the fuel runs out. In head-parity tokens (§1.3), a bit-0 digit (u,0,v) reads o^(u−1) z z a^(v−1), and the K block (10)^a 000 (10)^b reads o^(a−1) z z a^b. So a K block has the token shape of a digit (a,0,b+1). The deep digits look like frozen K blocks of a slower counter, and the T2/T3S2/Jc turns look like the places where a lower level hands a carry to a higher one.

Which turns the run uses, to 10^10: Lf 67,757, Lt 43, T2 44, T3S2 9, Jc 3. Lf is the ordinary carry into a bit-0 digit. The other 99 events are the turns at a junction, a bounce point or the blank, and these are where the invariant has to control the left text.

Suggested route (unchanged in spirit, now with exact rules): 1. Describe a configuration as a list of levels. Each level is a parent-style digit counter, delimited by a junction ((u,0,0), (0,0,0), 000) or by a bounce point (left cell 1 before a u = 1 digit). 2. For one level, lift the parent's F0–F3 arithmetic to G: Lf is the carry; Lt, T2, T3S2 and Jc are the level's overflows. 3. Find the inter-level invariant: what a level's overflow writes into the level above, and why that never produces an H or U ending. A solution here carries over to R4 by mirroring, as #33 did for #11.

Not assessed as a cryptid: no pseudo-random arithmetic has been seen, and the counts are powers of 2. Budget for this stage is used.

6. Halting unreachable, relative to the rules

  • Every G step is a composition of the windows of §2, so a G step never halts: halting is F reading 1, and the only F entries in the windows (T4, T9, T13) read 0 (check 3).
  • By §3.6, a K-configuration with a ≥ 1 that is in none of H1, H2, U0–U3 takes a G step to another such K-configuration with a' ≥ 1. Whether that one is again outside H and U is exactly §5.
  • So non-halting follows from an invariant as in §5, by induction over macro steps. Without §5 there is no proof.

About the coverage check (check 9). The window set contains 1–2-step windows (T11 = B0, S1–S4, S4c, T4, T7, T10). So a full greedy cover mostly says "did not halt", plus a few local bans: A0 is always followed by B1, and C0 D0 is never followed by A1. The review's own DP over the 23 windows also covers 1,024 of 1,342 random 150-step non-halting traces from random tapes. Check 9 shows only that the observed run uses no other local patterns. It is not evidence that unanalysed events are rule-like or safe, and not evidence for non-halting. The earlier sentence "any run that decomposes into these windows does not halt" was true but trivial, because F1 is in no window. The "other" events are now covered by the closed forms of §3.5, not by check 9.
