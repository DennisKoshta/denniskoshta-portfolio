Summary. The sweep boundary is S(w) = 0^inf 11 D> <w> 0^inf, as in the parent. The parent's tokens and passes carry over, with two changes: 1. At an L, this machine sends +1 to the rest but adds no pass. 2. There is a one-cell stray token S = 1, a drain suspended with 0 passes.

Call a boundary word w = L^n t good, G(w), if the n increments sent by its own leading L's turn t into a word with no stray. Then: - the sweep of a good word is defined and leaves at most one stray (Lemma C); - the next word is good exactly when the current sweep would leave no stray if it were given n' extra passes, where n' is the next word's L-run. That is one inequality, θ(x) ≤ n', for a threshold θ of an S-free word x (Lemma D).

What is missing is an invariant that implies this inequality at every sweep and is preserved by the sweep. On the first 3,000 sweeps of the abstract orbit (and at the 278 boundaries from blank to 1e9) the inequality holds with 2θ ≤ 0.962 n' at worst. But none of the candidates tried so far is preserved by the sweep for all words: not the bare inequality, not its factor-2 version, not that plus the orbit's value bound (with or without a lower bound on N), and not the V*-based J4 of §3.3.5.

1. Families and notation

  • Tokens: L = 110, 0 = 000, 1 = 100 (3 cells each, as in the parent) and the stray S = 1 (one cell). For a word w over {L,0,1,S}, <w> is its cells in order. A word followed by 0^inf denotes the same tape with or without trailing 0 tokens.
  • Canonical form: of a tape, the token word with the fewest S (parse in check_proof.py). It differs from a word produced by the clauses only at the right end: S 0^j 0^inf = 1 0^inf and S 1 0^inf = L 0^inf.
  • Value: V(x) is the little-endian binary number formed by the 0/1 tokens of an S-free word x. L tokens are skipped.
  • Family: S(w) := 0^inf 11 D> <w> 0^inf (busycoq S' r, with the token DS for S and toRC (DS::r) = [1] *> toRC r).
  • Passes (busycoq sideRLs), as in the parent:
  • h: B> X ->+ <C X';
  • h': 1 D> X ->+ <C X', where the 1 belongs to the pass.
  • A pass never reads a cell left of where it exits, so it holds for every left part.
  • Relations:
  • Inc(r,r'): the pass [h] turns <r> into <r'>.
  • R(hx,k,r,r'): the passes [h', h^k] turn <r> into <r'> (the eater plus k queued passes).
  • R(h1,k,r,r'): the passes [h^k] turn 1 <r> into <r'> (k passes draining over a stray 1).
  • Re-parse constructor (RIncs_eq, pass 3): R(tp,k,r0,r') /\ toRC r = toRC r0 -> R(tp,k,r,r'). It lets a derivation read its input in another token form of the same tape. Its spec case is one rewrite (§3.1). It is used in one place only, Lemma C (§3.3.3).
  • Functional shorthand (§3.3): E_k(r) is the r' of R(hx,k,r,r') and D_k(r) is the r' of R(h1,k,r,r'), when they exist. This is justified by Lemma U (§3.3.1), not by determinism of the machine. Under the convention "R0, not Z, on []", every instance without RIncs_eq has at most one derivation. A derivation that uses RIncs_eq is never compared with another one. "S-free" always refers to the canonical form.

3.3 The domain

3.3.1 Semantic reading, Lemma A and Lemma U

R and Inc are pass compositions (§1), so E_k = Inc^k ∘ E_0 and D_k(r) = Inc^k(S r) as tapes. The clauses then read as follows: - Inc(S 1 r) = L S r, Inc(S 0 r) = 0 S r, Inc(S L r) = L E_0(Inc r) and Inc(S) = 0 1. So a +1 pass moves the stray right by one token, or absorbs it at an L by starting a nested eater. - E_k(L r) = L E_k(Inc r), E_k(0 r) = L E_{k+1}(r) and E_k(1 r) = L D_k(r). - There is no clause for E_k([]), E_k(S..) or D_{k+1}(S..). These are the three failure modes. In the two stray cases, unless an L follows the stray, the machine leaves the token form.

Lemma A (one more pass is +1 on the result): R(tp,k,r,r') /\ Inc(r',r'') -> R(tp,k+1,r,r''). - Proof by induction on R, exactly as the parent's RIncs_Inc. - X1 and C1 use the IH at k+1. C3 and C4 swap (carry). R0 -> R1, R1 -> R2, R2 -> R1. - The new case Z: Inc(S::r, r'') can only be IS, whose premise is R(h1,1,r,r''). (There is no re-parse constructor for Inc, so this inversion stays valid.) - The new case EQ: the IH on R(tp,k,r0,r') gives R(tp,k+1,r0,r''), and EQ with the same tape identity gives R(tp,k+1,r,r''). - If the given derivation does not use EQ, neither does the one built, and neither does any Inc derivation it consumes.

Lemma U (uniqueness, pass 3). Convention: at k = 0 on [], every derivation built in this proof uses R0, never Z. (Z on [] would give [S], the same tape as R0's [1] by s0_eq 0, so nothing is lost.) Under this convention, Inc and the EQ-free R are partial functions: every instance Inc(r,·) or R(tp,k,r,·) has at most one derivation, whatever the input, strays included. - Proof by mutual induction on derivations, inverting the second one. Every clause is fixed by (tp, k, first token): - Inc: L gives I3, 0 gives I2, 1 gives I1, [] gives I4, and S gives IS. - hx: L gives X1, 0 gives X2, 1 gives X3. - h1 with k = 0: Z, or R0 on [] (the convention). - h1 with k ≥ 1: L gives C1, 1 gives C2, 0 gives C3 or C4 by the parity of k, and [] gives R1 or R2 by parity. - The Inc premise of X1/C1 and the R premise of IS are unique by the IH. So the next premise's input is the same word in both derivations. - Lemmas A and B respect the convention. Lemma A turns R0 into R1 with premise R0. Its Z case reuses the premise of the given IS. Its other cases recurse. Lemma B uses R0 on []. - So Lemma A's output is the output of R(tp,k+1,r,·), syntactically. - This is what the shorthand E_k, D_k, the chain in §3.3.3 and the threshold θ below rely on. The parent used only Inc's functionality; here IS embeds R in Inc, so U covers both. - EQ is outside U. It is used once per sweep (Lemma C), on a derivation that is never compared with another. - Determinism of the machine is not used. sideRL quantifies over all left halves, so it is not obviously functional.

End identities commute with Inc (pass 3). For any u: Inc(u S 0^j) and Inc(u 1) are the same tape, and so are Inc(u S 1 0^j) and Inc(u L). - If the carry stops inside u, the right ends are untouched. - Otherwise the carry reaches the end: - IS with C3 then Z gives 0 S 0^(j-1), the same tape as 0 1, which is I1 then I4. For j = 0 it is R1 then R0, giving 0 1. - IS with C2 then Z gives L S 0^j, the same tape as L 1, which is I3 then I4. - So "S-free" (always the canonical form) does not depend on whether an Inc chain is re-parsed before or after.

3.3.2 Lemma B: S-free words are safe

For an S-free word r: - (B1) R(h1,k,r,·) exists for every k. - (B2) R(hx,k,r,·) exists for every k if and only if r contains a 1 or an L (r is not in 0*). - (B3) Each such output has at most one stray. - (B4) Inc of a word with at most one stray exists and has at most one stray.

Proof: - B1–B3 are proved by well-founded induction on the pair (|r|, number of L tokens in r), ordered lexicographically, together with an inner induction on k for R(h1,k,[]) (TM7's RIncs_nxt_1). - X2, X3, C2, C3, C4 recurse on a strictly shorter word. - X1 and C1 recurse on Inc(r) from L::r: - If the carry stops inside r, |Inc r| = |r| < |L::r|. - If it runs off the end, then r is over {L,1}, |Inc r| = |L::r|, and Inc r has one L fewer than L::r. - No stuck case. With no stray, the only clause-less case is the eater at the right end. E_k(0 r) = L E_{k+1}(r) reaches it exactly when r is in 0*. After an L the rest is Inc(..), which always contains a 1. - Stray count. Every clause emits one non-S token before its recursive output, except Z (S::r with r S-free) and R0. - B4. If the carry reaches the stray, Inc becomes IS, R(h1,1,v,·) on the S-free rest v, and B1 and B3 apply.

3.3.3 The predicate G and Lemma C

Let w = L^n t be a boundary word with n maximal. Assume w is canonical and has at most one stray; B4 needs both. Let t_n be the output of the chain Inc(t, t_1), Inc(t_1, t_2), …, Inc(t_(n-1), t_n). B4 says the chain exists, and U says it is unique (under the R0 convention). Let x(w) be the canonical form of t_n.

Definition. G(w) holds when w is canonical, #S(w) ≤ 1, and x(w) is S-free and contains a 1 or an L.

Lemma C. If G(w), then R(hx,0,w, L^n E_0(x)) is derivable with exactly one use of EQ, at the place given below. The output has at most one stray.

Proof (the derivation, in order): 1. X1, n times: R(hx,0,L^n t, L^n o) <- Inc(t, t_1), R(hx,0, L^(n-1) t_1, L^(n-1) o) and so on. The Inc premise of the i-th X1 is Inc(L^(n-i) t_(i-1), L^(n-i) t_i), by I3 over the remaining L's and then the chain above. This leaves the goal R(hx,0, t_n, o). 2. EQ here, once: R(hx,0, t_n, o) <- toRC t_n = toRC x, R(hx,0, x, o). This is the point after the eater's n increments and before it reads the tail. - t_n and x differ only at the right end. x is S-free, so the stray of t_n (if any) stands in S 0^j or S 1 0^j at the end (§1). - So the identity is s0_eq or s1_eq behind a common prefix, plus d0s_eq for trailing 0 tokens. 3. R(hx,0, x, o) exists by B2, because x is S-free and not in 0*. That B-derivation does not use EQ, and o has at most one stray by B3.

Why EQ is needed here, and not only between sweeps: take w = LS01, for which G holds (x = 0L). The only derivation of Inc(S01,·) is IS, C3, Z, which gives 0S1. The eater then eats the 0 (X2) and meets the S with k = 1, and no clause applies. With EQ, 0S1 is re-read as 0L and the sweep gives LLLL01, which matches the real machine. This derivation is the compiled Example sweep_LS01 in TwinSpec.v. Without EQ, 75 of the 15,593 G words with tails of at most 6 tokens and n ≤ 15 have no derivation; with EQ, all of them do (check 9a). None of the 3,000 orbit boundaries needs it, but the proof cannot rely on that.

After the sweep, the output L^n o is re-parsed canonically at the configuration level: S(L^n o) = S(w') with w' canonical, a tape identity (§3.2). w' has at most one stray by B3, so the first two conjuncts of G(w') hold automatically.

G(1) holds: n = 0 and x = 1.

3.3.4 Lemma D: the domain reduces to one inequality per sweep

Let G(w), let x = x(w), and let a be the number of leading L's of E_0(x). Then the next boundary word is w' = L^(n+a) t', where E_0(x) = L^a t'.

Lemma D. G(w') holds if and only if E_(n+a)(x) is S-free.

Proof: - Apply Lemma A n+a times: E_(n+a)(x) = Inc^(n+a)(L^a t') = L^a Inc^(n+a)(t'), the second step by I3. - The Inc steps here are w''s own chain (§3.3.3), behind a prefix of L's (I3). So x(w') is, up to the canonical re-parse, the tail of this E_(n+a)(x). - By U, this is the (unique) derivation of R(hx,n+a,x,·). - E_(n+a)(x) exists by B2. - w' is canonical with at most one stray (§3.3.3). So G(w') is equivalent to: x(w') is S-free and contains a 1 or an L. The S-free part is the claim. - The "1 or L" part holds automatically (needed for B2 at the next sweep): - a ≥ 1, because X1, X2 and X3 all emit L as their first token. So n' = n + a ≥ 1, and x(w') is the output of at least one Inc. - Every Inc output contains a 1 or an L: - I2 and I4 write a 1, and I3 keeps its L. - I1 outputs 0 :: followed by an Inc output, so the claim holds by induction. - IS runs R(h1,1,r,·). Its first clause is C1 or C2, which emit L. Otherwise it is C3 followed by Z, giving 0 S r, whose re-parse, when S-free, is 0 1 0^j or 0 L 0^j. Otherwise (r = []) it is R1 then R0, giving 0 1. C4 needs k ≥ 2. - Canonical re-parsing does not change the tape, and an S-free word whose tape has a 1-cell has a token 1 or L. - Check 9b confirms this on every word of check 9a.

Threshold. For S-free x not in 0*, let θ(x) be the least M such that E_M(x) is S-free. - It exists. Until the first Z, the sequence of clauses and remaining inputs does not depend on the counts: only the bits written by C3/C4 and the end digits do. By the measure of Lemma B this sequence has finitely many steps, and each step maps a count c to at least (c-2)/2. So a large enough M never reaches 0. - It is monotone. E_(M+1) = Inc ∘ E_M (Lemma A, with U for "the" output). Inc maps S-free words to S-free words (I1–I4), and it commutes with the canonical re-parse (§3.3.1). - It is well defined. By U, E_M(x) is a single word. The check scripts compute θ by doubling and binary search, which relies on monotonicity. Check 9c also computes it by brute-force linear search for its family.

So G(w') <-> θ(x) <= n' with n' = n + a. The next tail is x' = x(w') = drop_a(E_(n')(x)).

Writing N_s := n_(s+1) for the sweep s with S-free word x_s, the whole domain question is this. For the dynamical system

(N, x)  |->  (N + a(x'), x')      with   x' := E_N(x) minus its first a(x) L's,   a(y) := leading L's of E_0(y)

started from the orbit, show θ(x) <= N at every step.

3.3.5 Open: an inductive invariant

What is needed is a predicate J(N, x) that: - holds at some reachable boundary (the start can be moved later by computation); - implies θ(x) <= N; - satisfies J(N, x) -> J(N + a(x'), x').

Refuted candidates (each counterexample is checked in §7, checks 8f, 9c and 9i): - G itself, as a predicate on words. 1001 satisfies G, and its sweep LS001 does not: Inc(S001) = 0S01, and then the eater meets the stray. Among words with G (tails of at most 5 tokens, n ≤ 13), 181 of 1,893 sweep to a word without G. - J0 := θ(x) ≤ N. Take x = 00010001 and N = 5. The reviewer's random search found the same: about 25,000 sampled states, none violating θ' ≤ N'.

Pass 3: a weighted value that makes the sweep affine. For an S-free word y, define V*(y): the little-endian binary value of its 0/1 tokens, plus 2^p for every L that stands after exactly p digit tokens. So an L counts as the +1 it sends to the rest when eaten. For example, V(1L01) = 1 + 2 + 0 + 4 = 7. - (V1, proved.) Inc adds exactly 1 to V* on S-free words. - I1/I2 are binary +1 on the digit tokens. I3 does not change how many digits stand before any L. I4 appends a 1 after all the L's, at position p (the number of digits): every earlier digit was a 1 and turned to 0, so the total gains 2^p − (2^p − 1) = 1 in digits, and the L weights are unchanged. - Check 9e: exhaustive, words up to 8 tokens. - (V2, proved from Lemma A, U and V1.) The sweep is affine. For S-free x not in 0*, let y(x) be the canonical form of E_θ(x) without its first a(x) L's (an S-free word), and let c(x) := V(y(x)) − θ(x). Then for every N ≥ θ(x):

V*(x') = N + c(x), x' = Inc^(N−θ)(y(x)), and the L's of x' are exactly the L's of y(x), at the same digit positions

  • Lemma A gives E_N = Inc^(N−θ) ∘ E_θ. I3 carries every Inc past the a leading L's. The canonical re-parse of the tail can be done before the Incs (end identities commute with Inc, §3.3.1). Then apply V1.
  • Check 9f: exhaustive up to 7 tokens, 12 values of N each. Along the orbit the law holds on all 2,999 sweeps (9h).
  • (Lemma T, CONJECTURE.) 2θ(x) ≤ V*(x) − 1 for every S-free x that ends with the token 1.
  • Exhaustive up to 11 tokens (check 9g, all 88,573 such words).
  • It is tight: 1 0^m 1 has 2θ = V* − 3.
  • It is false for words ending with L, for example 100000000L has θ = 511 and V* = 513. So "ends with 1" is part of any J built on T.
  • At every x_s of the orbit, T holds (9h).
  • Plausible proof route, not done: induction on the clause chain of E_θ(x). Track Φ := (current count) · 2^(digit position) plus the V* of the unread rest. A drain step on a 0 halves the count while the position moves up by one, a 1 costs one pass, and an L hands over to the eater, which only gains.
  • Consequence. Under T, the domain inequality θ(x) ≤ N follows from J4(N, x) := x ends with 1 and V*(x) ≤ 2N + 1. By V2, J4 is preserved exactly when c(x) ≤ N + 2a(x') + 1 and x' still ends with 1.
  • J4 is not inductive (check 9i). Take (N, x) = (257, 10000000L1): V* = 513 ≤ 515 and θ = 255.
  • The drain reaches the L with 1 pass. The eater restarted there turns every remaining token into an L at digit position 7.
  • So c(x) = 385, x' = 0100000LLL01, N' = 259, and V*(x') = 642 > 519.
  • But θ(x') = 94 ≤ N', and the system goes on: the domain itself is not violated.
  • But J4 is a strong attractor (check 9j). From 400 random J4 states (N ≥ 131, biased to V* ≈ 2N, many with an L just below the top bit), the system ran 25 sweeps each, and θ ≤ N held at every one of the 10,000 steps.
  • From the third state on, V*/N stayed below about 1.45.
  • Along the orbit, for s ≥ 27: 0.952 ≤ V*(x_s)/N_s ≤ 1.193, and c(x_s) ≤ 0.215 N_s (9h).
  • Where c(x) comes from.
  • For pure binary x (no L), c(x) ≤ |x|: every one of the 2,047 such words up to 11 tokens (check 9g).
  • Large c comes only from L's that the drain reaches with few passes left. There the restarted eater turns the rest into L's at that position. Examples: 10000000L1 has c = 385 with V = 513, and L0000LLL1 has c = 57 with V = 65 (check 9g).
  • No bound of the form c ≤ k·Λ(x) + |x| holds for k = 2, 3 or 4, where Λ(x) is the L part of V*. For example, 1000L11111 has c = 89 with Λ = 16. A fixed linear bound in Λ is not enough, because the 1's after the L matter too.

What is open, precisely. Find a shape condition K(N, x), such that: 1. K(N, x) and x ends with 1 imply c(x) ≤ N + 3. With V2, this keeps V* ≤ 2N + 1. 2. K and J4 together give "x' ends with 1" and K(N', x'). 3. K holds at some reachable boundary.

Then J := J4 ∧ K, together with a proof of Lemma T, closes the domain. - K can only talk about where the L's of x sit relative to its top digit and how many passes reach them. By V2, the L's of x' are those of y(x), and the digits of x' are y(x)'s plus N − θ. - So K is a statement about the threshold output y(x) against the count N − θ that is added to it. - Two-step or "eventually" forms (J4 ∪ sweep(J4)) are an alternative. In all 400 samples of 9j, two sweeps brought the state back to V* ≤ 1.45 N.

What the orbit does (checked in §7, check 8e; these are the first 3,000 sweeps, not the whole orbit): - G holds at every boundary of the 3,000-sweep abstract orbit and at all 278 from-blank boundaries to 1e9. - x_s always ends with the token 1, so the top bit slot is filled. - 2θ(x_s) ≤ 0.962 N_s. The worst case is sweep 27: N = 131 and θ = 63. - V(x_s) + (leading L's of x_s) ≤ 1.25 N_s. The bound is attained with equality at sweep 2 (N = 4, x = 101: V = 5), so J3's constant 5/4 has no slack at all (check 9d). Any reuse needs a slack constant or a start after sweep 27. - V(x_(s+1)) = N_s - θ(x_s) exactly on 2,813 of 2,999 sweeps. Equivalently, the threshold output E_θ(x_s) has value 0, since Inc is +1 on V for S-free words. So each sweep "adds N and eats from the bottom". - V2 is the exact form of this, with the L's counted: V*(x_(s+1)) = N_s + c(x_s) on all 2,999 sweeps.

Why the parent's method does not transfer directly. The parent's key lemma tracks only counts (tp, k, j, tq, q) and works for every input word. - Here the second sweep's count does not grow when it eats an L. The first sweep's view count (k + j) grows instead. - So a second-sweep drain that starts from a 1 bit can meet a view count far larger than its own, and run out. - Whether that happens depends on how many 0 tokens lie ahead, which is a property of the word, not of the counts. - Any working J must therefore bound the shape of the tail, not only counts. Pass 3 narrows this: under V2 and Lemma T, the counts reduce to the single inequality V* ≤ 2N + 1. What remains of the shape is the condition K on the L's.

This is the remaining mathematics. It is not a cryptid: every rule is counter arithmetic (+1, halving, bijective binary), and V2 makes the counts exactly affine. But the closing argument is a new invariant (K plus a proof of Lemma T), not a delta of the parent's.

3.4 Progress

R is inductive (mutually with Inc), so every derivation is finite, and Lemma B's measure bounds the clause sequence. Each sweep is a finite run of at least 4 steps: h' takes at least 1 and the wall takes 3.

Every rule is a proved exact run, so no step reaches F1. Here F is entered only by E0 = 0RF, because A1 no longer enters F. In every clause run of §7, F never read a 1.

5. Conclusion: blocked on §3.3.5

Given J, the assembly is the parent's: 1. multistep_nonhalt to the starting boundary (§4). 2. progress_nonhalt_cond with

P(w) := w canonical /\ #S(w) <= 1 /\ G(w) /\ J(n(w) + a(x(w)), x(w))

The first two conjuncts are also inside G (§3.3.3). They are written out because B4, x(w) and the S-free test all need them, and P must carry them from one sweep to the next. 3. Without J, the machine is not shown to run forever. The candidate from pass 3 is J := J4 /\ K with Lemma T (§3.3.5). In it, J4 is a pure count condition and K is the still-unknown condition on where the L's sit.
