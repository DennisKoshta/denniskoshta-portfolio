0. What the machine does

The tape is a binary counter that is fed at its left end. Every time the head reaches the left edge in state C, it walks right as B, and that walk is one carry (an "arrival"). The carry runs through a word of short blocks called units until a unit absorbs it, and then C walks back to the left edge.

Most units are passive, but one kind, the wall, is a rotor. A wall absorbs every second carry it receives. On the other carries it sends three carries further right and then moves one unit to the right. So each wall forwards 3/2 as many carries as it receives.

The left end (the nursery, a short gadget word) is exactly periodic. Once per period it puts 8 new walls into the unit word. It touches the unit word only by acting as a wall itself. The walls ride a conveyor of P blocks, in order, and at the right end they are absorbed one by one into a zone. The zone is a binary counter with a few marker units.

The machine can halt only if a wall's "shift" (a one-cell lattice offset that a wall leaves behind when it moves) lands directly on another wall-like unit. The proof shows that this never happens: - in the conveyor, because three carries always turn the unit above a firing wall into P (Lemmas C1, C2); - in the zone, because the zone input is a fixed closed system (Lemma T), and that system never keeps a zone wall alive across an absorption (Theorem B).

1. Notation

Tape notation follows busycoq: 0^inf, B> (head in state B reading the cell to its right), <C (head in state C reading the cell to its left). Words are written left to right.

Units. A unit is a cell word h 1000, where the head h is a 0/1 word with no 000 inside it, or the 0 digit 0000.

name cells head h role
D0 0000 (0 digit) counter digit 0
D1 1000 ε counter digit 1
P 11000 1 pass block (wire)
S1 01000 0 shifted digit
SP 011000 01 wall, state W0 (absorbs the next carry)
W 111000 11 wall, state W1 (forwards 3 carries)

A unit word is a finite concatenation of units followed by 0^inf. Since D0 0^inf = 0^inf, trailing D0 units are part of the blank. So we always take a unit word in normal form: its last unit is not D0 (the empty word is the blank). - In normal form the tokenisation is unique: read zeros in groups of 4 as D0, and the leftover zeros (0 to 3 of them) join the head of the next unit. - Unit indices, digit positions and equality of zones are all read in normal form. - A written form that ends in D0 units (for example ... D0 bin(q) with q = 0, where bin(0) is the empty word) stands for its normal form, with those D0 units removed.

L_gap is the set of unit words whose heads are all in {ε, 0, 1, 01, 11}, that is, only the six units above. Deeper heads (001, 011, 111, 101, 0111, 1111, 0101, 1101, ...) are called stacked.

Shift. When a W fires it leaves one extra 0 cell above it. Re-tokenised, that 0 passes over any D0 units and prepends a 0 to the head of the first other unit above it. We say the shift lands on that unit: - on D1 it gives S1, and on P it gives SP; both are in L_gap; - on S1, SP or W it gives the heads 00, 001 or 011. This is a hit, and it leaves L_gap; - if only D0 units and the blank lie above, the 0 merges into the blank.

inc. inc(w) is the unit word after one carry enters w at its left end (§2.3).

Indices. inc keeps the normal form: a carry turns a unit into D0 only when it passes the carry on upward, so the last unit is never left as D0. A carry that passes the last unit appends a D1. A shift only prepends a 0 to a head, or merges into the blank. - So, in normal form, inc never changes the number of units of a word, except that a carry reaching the blank top appends one D1. - Every unit therefore keeps a fixed integer index for as long as it exists. Units added at the left end (§4) get smaller indices. - S12 checks the normal form at every step of the closed-system orbit (no zone ever ends in D0).

2.2 Unit lemmas (S2: proofs by oracle simulation)

Arrival. Write Arr(w) = w' (in s steps) for

l B> w 0^inf   -->(s)   l <C w' 0^inf

for every left context l, where the head never moves left of w's first cell until the last step, and the last step moves it off w to the left in state C.

Oracle proof. Each unit lemma below is proved by running the machine on the unit's own cells alone, with the rest r of the word replaced by an oracle: - When the head steps onto r's first cell, it must be in state B. The hypothesis Arr(r) = r' then returns the head to the unit's last cell in state C, with r replaced by r'. - Any other contact with r, or a halt, fails the lemma.

These are finite computations. check_proof.py S2 runs all of them, and the chains are printed below.

Here "shift(x)" means the extra 0 cell 0 x, re-tokenised as in §1. The cell identity h 1000 0 x = h 1000 . shift(x) is pure re-reading, so no machine step is involved. The W lemma holds for every left context l, and §4 uses this.

What a hit does (aside, not used). The main argument excludes every hit. A hit on SP or W gives the stacked heads 001 or 011, which have rows above. A hit on S1 gives head 00. Its next carry meets head 10, which has no unit rule (oracle: 101000 -> 011100 in 7 steps, which takes a cell from the unit above). A shift that lands on 001 gives 0001, which is not a valid head. So a stacked unit can leave the unit calculus in more than one way, and the proof never lets one arise.

2.3 Theorem U (arrival = inc) (S3)

Define the relation Inc(w, w', s) ("one carry turns w into w' in s machine steps") inductively by the table in §2.2: - Inc(0^inf, D1, 1) (the top case); - first cell 0: Inc(0 x, 1 x, 1); - D1: if Inc(r, r', s) then Inc(D1 . r, D0 . r', 8 + s); - P: if Inc(r, r', s) then Inc(P . r, P . r', 10 + s); - W: if Inc(r, r1, s1), Inc(r1, r2, s2) and Inc(r2, r3, s3), then Inc(W . r, P . shift(r3), 34 + s1 + s2 + s3); - the stacked heads 111, 101, 1111 and 1101 follow their rows in the same way (κ = 25, 14, 27, 16).

inc(w) is defined when some (then unique) w', s with Inc(w, w', s) exist. That is, the recursion is finite and never meets a head outside the table.

Theorem U. If Inc(w, w', s), then Arr(w) = w' in exactly s machine steps.

Proof. Induction on the derivation of Inc(w, w', s). The last rule used is the unit lemma of w's first unit (§2.2). Its oracle calls are exactly the premises of that rule, which are smaller derivations. So the induction hypothesis turns each oracle call into a real machine run, ending on the unit's last cell in state C as the oracle requires. Re-tokenising after a shift is a cell identity. ∎

(The earlier version inducted on the cell length of w. That fails: W's second and third oracle calls are on inc(r) and inc²(r), which need not be shorter than w.)

Lemma U-term (termination). Let w ∈ L_gap have n units, and suppose no W firing inside the recursion for inc(w) is a hit. Then inc(w) is defined.

Proof. We prove a stronger statement by induction on the number of walls (SP and W units) of w:

If w ∈ L_gap and no W firing inside the recursion for inc(w) is a hit, then inc(w) is defined, inc(w) ∈ L_gap, and inc(w) has at most as many walls as w.

  1. No wall creation. The rows never raise the wall count. SP becomes W on a carry. W becomes P when it fires, and its shift turns at most one unit above into a wall (P into SP); on D1 or the blank it makes none. D1, D0, P and S1 never become walls by a carry.
  2. Base: w has no wall. Its units are D0, D1, P and S1. Each forwards at most one carry (D1, P) or stops it (D0, S1). So an arrival is one upward path of at most n + 1 unit steps, and the top step appends a D1. inc(w) is in L_gap and has no wall.
  3. Step: w has walls. Write w = x · V · y, where V is the lowest wall and x is wall-free. - The carry climbs x as in the base case. It stops in x, or it reaches V. - If V = SP, it becomes W and the carry stops. - If V = W, the row needs inc(y), inc(inc(y)) and inc³(y). Here y has fewer walls than w, so inc(y) is defined, lies in L_gap, and has no more walls than y (induction hypothesis). The same holds for inc(y) and then for inc²(y). These nested recursions are part of the recursion for inc(w), so they have no hit either. - The shift then lands on D1 (making S1), on P (making SP) or on the blank. A hit is excluded by assumption. - In every case inc(w) ∈ L_gap, and its wall count is at most that of w (item 1). ∎

The earlier proof claimed that the region above the old top holds only digits. That is false: one arrival on W W SP gives P SP P SP D0 D0 D1 (237 machine steps), with an SP above the old top. The induction on walls does not need that claim. S3 checks the wall count bound on every L_gap word it runs (walls(inc w) ≤ walls(w)).

Check S3 compares the machine and inc, cells and exact step counts, on every unit word of up to 4 units over 14 unit kinds (quick mode: 3 units) and on 20,000 random words with long P runs: 0 mismatches. A RecursionError in the model now counts as a failure.

Corollary U'. Let w ∈ L_gap, and suppose no W firing inside the recursion for inc(w) is a hit. Then inc(w) is defined and inc(w) ∈ L_gap. Proof: the L_gap rows produce only L_gap heads, except for the shift. A shift lands on D1 (giving S1), on P (giving SP), on the blank, or is a hit. Termination is Lemma U-term. ∎

So the machine halts only after a hit. The halting transition A1 is never used by any unit lemma: in every oracle proof, state A reads 0.

3. The conveyor cannot be hit (Lemmas C1 and C2)

Lemma C1. If the first unit of x is P, S1, SP or W, then inc³(x) starts with P.

Proof. What happens to the first unit depends only on that unit, because its carries and its shift go to the units above. P stays P. S1 becomes P after one carry. SP becomes W, then fires and becomes P. W fires and becomes P. P then stays P. ∎ (S5 also runs the four cases.)

Lemma C2 (no hit below the zone). A W firing whose unit directly above is P, S1, SP or W is not a hit. Its shift lands on that unit, which is P after the 3 carries (C1), so it becomes SP: the wall has moved up one unit. Moreover, a prefix of a unit word made of P, SP and W units stays so under inc, and can only grow: these units only turn into P, SP or W, and a zone unit above can only join by becoming P. ∎

So a hit needs a firing W whose unit directly above is a digit. The conveyor (§4) has no digits.

Lemma C2 replaces the earlier "interior wires" Lemma I. That statement was wrong: a W directly followed by S1, SP or W is harmless.

4. The nursery (Lemma N; proved by a finite window computation, S5)

Left records and gadgets. A left record is a time at which the head stands on a cell never visited before, at the left end. From step 203,465 on, the records come in a fixed cycle of 8, XC XD XE YC YD YE ZD ZE (record group, then state). At a record the tape is

0^inf <q G U 0^inf

Here the gadget G is the first three 1-runs after the head cell together with the 3 cells after them. U is the rest, read as a unit word.

The window computation. Run the machine on the head cell and G only. The rest R (initially U) is opaque. The run may touch it only through two proved rules: - (arr) The head steps onto R's first cell in state B. By Theorem U, R becomes inc(R) and the head is back on the window's last cell in state C. - (W) The head stands in state B on the first of the window's last six cells, and they read 111000. The W lemma (valid for every left context) applies with r = R: R receives three carries, the six cells become 110000, and the trailing 0 is the shift. By Lemma C1, inc³(R) starts with P if R's first unit was not a digit, so the shift makes that P an SP. Its five cells 11000 move from R into the window (a pop), and R loses its first unit.

Any other contact with R, or a halt, would fail the computation. The run ends when the head steps onto a new cell left of the window, which is the next left record. The window's cells after the new gadget must then read as units in {P, SP, W}. Call them F; they are put in front of R.

Each step is the window run, whose contacts with R are justified by Theorem U, the W lemma and Lemma C1. ∎

5. The top relation (Lemma T; proved by a flux identity, S6) and the closed zone system

Zone and absorptions. The top wall is the highest conveyor wall. The zone bottom z is the index of the first unit above it that is not P. The units from z up form the zone Z, and the P units between the top wall and z form its wire.

Absorption j (j = 0, 1, 2, …) is a firing of the top wall after which the wall does not move up. That is, after the three carries the unit directly above the wall is not P. Then the wall was at z − 1, and its shift lands in the zone: - on a digit at or above z (a D1 directly above the wall becomes S1; this is the most common case), or - on a zone P above some D0 units (it becomes SP, a zone wall).

If instead the three carries turn the unit above into P (a drop, z += 1), the shift lands on that P and the wall moves up one unit.

Let N_j be the number of carries that enter the zone between absorptions j−1 and j. Let A_j be the number of P units that leave the zone bottom in that interval (drops; each one is z += 1). Let D_j = A_1 + … + A_(j−1). Nothing else moves z, so z = z_0 + (drops so far).

Lemma T. For every j ≥ 1:

N_j = 3*A_j + D_j + p(j mod 8),      p = (47, 48, 48, 47, 48, 47, 48, 47)   for j mod 8 = 0..7

Proof. Number the walls in birth order. The 6 walls of U_0 come first; they count as born at the step-203,465 record, with b their index there and s = 1 for W. The births of §4 follow. By FIFO, absorption j removes wall number j.

For a wall w let b_w be its birth index, s_w ∈ {0, 1} its birth state (SP or W), and R_w(t) the carries it has received since birth. A wall fires on every second carry, so it has fired ⌊(R_w + s_w)/2⌋ times and sits at index b_w + ⌊(R_w + s_w)/2⌋.

  1. Wall j exists before absorption j−1. Wall j is born at the same record as wall j−1, or at the end of the next excursion. Before then, wall j−1 receives only that excursion's carries (at most 36). Its absorption needs 2(z − b_(j−1)) − s ≥ 2·33 − 1 carries, since z ≥ 43 and b ≤ 10.
  2. Wall j−1's carries. Let H_(j−1) be the carries wall j−1 received before wall j's birth. After that, every carry it receives comes from wall j, because carries only move up and wall j is the next wall below it. With E_j(t) the carries wall j has emitted since its birth, R_(j−1) = H_(j−1) + E_j.
  3. At absorption j−1. Wall j−1 fires from index z(t_(j−1)) − 1. That is its (z(t_(j−1)) − b_(j−1))-th firing, so R_(j−1) = 2(z(t_(j−1)) − b_(j−1)) − s_(j−1). This firing happens inside a triple of wall j. Say wall j has fired f times (this triple included), with r ∈ {0, 1, 2} carries of the triple still to come. Then E_j = 3f − r.
  4. Interval j. Wall j is now the top wall, so every carry that enters the zone comes from it: the r leftover carries, then 3 per further firing. Wall j is absorbed when it fires from z(t_j) − 1, after z(t_j) − b_j firings in all. So N_j = r + 3(z(t_j) − b_j − f).
  5. Substitute 3f − r from items 2 and 3, and use z(t_j) − z(t_(j−1)) = A_j and z(t_(j−1)) = z(t_0) + D_j:

6. The zone never gets a hit (Theorem B)

Alive walls. A zone wall is alive at an absorption if, just before an absorption shift, Z contains a unit whose head is not ε, 0 or 1 (that is, a unit other than D0, D1, S1 and P). This includes SP, W, the head 00 and every stacked head.

A step is defined if every carry of its carry phase meets a head that has a row (§2.3). A run that reaches a canonical state (below) is defined at every step.

Two facts about single carries are used below. - (F1) A non-digit unit never becomes a digit unit: every row maps a head to a nonempty head, and a shift only prepends a 0. A non-digit unit leaves Z only by a drop. - (F2) An S1 that receives a carry becomes P. Only a shift landing on a D1 makes a new S1.

Lemma Z1 (the absorption shift never lands on S1). Take a defined step of the closed system with feed c ≥ 1. At the end of its carry phase, the first non-D0 unit of Z is not an S1.

Proof. Suppose the first non-D0 unit u is an S1 at the end. By (F2), u has received no carry since it became an S1. Let E be the last of these events in the step: its start, the creation of u (if u is created during the step), or a drop. - By (F1), a non-digit unit below u after E would still be below u at the end, where only D0 units are. So after E every unit below u is a digit unit. They form a counter of some r digits at the bottom of Z, with value x right after E. - Every carry fed after E enters this counter, and none reaches u. With t such carries, x + t < 2^r, and the counter ends at 0. So x = t = 0.

Each choice of E contradicts this: - E is the start of the step. Then t = c ≥ 1. - E is a drop. A drop raises the number of carries the phase feeds by 3, and when it happens the carries fed so far do not exceed the old number. So t ≥ 3. - E is the creation of u. A wall's shift made u, and that wall is P right after it fires, below u. By (F1) this P must leave Z by a drop later, so E was not the last event.

The feed is c = D + p ≥ 47, so the lemma applies to every step. ∎

Lemma Z0 (no alive wall, no hit). Take a run of the closed system in which every step is defined and no zone wall is alive at any absorption. Then no shift of the run is a hit, and every head stays in L_gap.

Proof. - Absorption shifts. By hypothesis the first non-D0 unit is D1, S1 or P (or there is none, and the shift merges into the blank). By Lemma Z1 it is not S1. So the shift makes an S1 or an SP, or it merges into the blank. - At most one wall. Right after an absorption at most one unit lies outside {D0, D1, S1, P}: the SP just born, if the shift landed on a P. Carries never turn D0, D1, S1 or P into a wall. A wall's shift that lands on a P only moves that same wall up. So there is never a second wall. - Wall shifts. The single wall's shift lands on the first non-D0 unit above it, after its 3 carries: - on a P: the same wall moves up one unit; - on a D1: it becomes S1, and the wall is dissolved; - on the blank: the wall is dissolved; - on an S1: the head 00 appears. If it then receives a carry it becomes 10, which has no row, so the step is not defined. Otherwise it is still there at the next absorption, where it is alive. Both are excluded. - SP and W cannot be landed on, because the only wall is the emitter, and a shift lands above its emitter. ∎

6.4 Endgame by locality (Lemma L; proved, S10)

Digit positions and value. In a zone, D0 and D1 are the digit units. The digit position of a unit is the number of digit units below it. V(Z) is the sum of 2^(position) over the D1 units; P, S1, SP and W carry no weight. Above the top of a zone the blank reads as D0 digits.

Members. Let s = (Z, D, j) be a closed-system state, the representative, and let R ≥ 5 and Δ ≡ 0 (mod 2^R). A state s′ = (Z′, D′, j′) is an (R, Δ)-member of s if: - (M1) j′ − j = D′ − D = Δ. Then the feeds differ by c′ − c = Δ at every step, since p depends on j mod 8. - (M2) Z = X·Y and Z′ = X·Y′, where X runs up to and including the digit unit at position R − 1, and Y, Y′ consist of digit units only. - (M3) V(Z′) − V(Z) = Δ. - (M4) At every step, the member's feed c′ is at least T0, a bound fixed with the representative.

Lemma L (locality). Run the representative with κ_0 = 1. At the start of step t, set the cut r_t = R + min(0, ν2(κ_t)) digit units. Suppose that at every step: - (H0) every non-digit unit of Z has at most r_t − 1 digit units below it. So it lies in X, the part of Z up to and including the digit unit at position r_t − 1. - (H1) every shift landing in the step (wall shifts and the absorption shift), on a digit or on a non-digit unit alike, is on a unit with at most r − 1 digit units below it. Here r starts at r_t and drops by one at each landing on a D1, because that D1 becomes an S1 and X has one digit unit fewer. Such a unit lies at or below a digit unit of X, so every landing is on a unit of X. - (H2) T0 ≤ c, and after the first T0 carries of the step, the zone has no unit other than D0, D1 and P.

(H0) and (H1) are meant in this strict sense: "at most r − 1 digit units below", not "at most r". The checks (locality.py, vf.py) test exactly this, for digit and non-digit targets alike.
