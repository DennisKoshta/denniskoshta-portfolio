Mechanism

How it works: 3 notes
  • Left: a binary counter in 3-cell blocks (1=100, 0=000, filler f=010 that carries skip). Right: tm10's 011 unary run with a 00 marker. Each round adds 1 and moves the marker one block left.
  • When the marker reaches the counter, one of 3 eats fires. EAT0f drops the low digit and fillers into the unary. EAT0z and EAT1 absorb the marker and shift the counter one cell right (phi).
  • The shifted blocks are debris (s=001). A carry that reaches an s re-encodes them (CARRY_S, recursive). On the blank run the debris is always gone before the next eat.

Proof strategy & riskshow the formal proof will go · 3 risks to watch

Proof on round starts RS/NM, with W over {1,0,f,s}. Invariant: no 1 left of an s, and no s at an eat (the flush lemma). Unary lemmas from BECv1; inc/incC/CARRY_S as an inductive carry relation in BECv7 style. Then progress_nonhalt_cond. The open piece is the flush lemma: before the marker returns, the counter carries through every s block.

  • ▲The flush lemma is a size bound (2^(digits right of the leftmost s) < epoch length, about m+1); it holds with slack on the blank run (worst ratio 0.44, below 0.11 after 1e9) but is not proved.
  • ▲An eat with s still present leaves the block alphabet; random words that do this can halt (26 of 1,200 fuzz samples), so the invariant must carry the bound.
  • ▲CARRY_S is recursive (debris inside debris), so the Coq carry relation must be mutually inductive, like BECv7's RIncs.
