3. Rules (digit level; absim.py docstring). One bell-1 pass is inc(field 2). - inc on a rest digit: v < 2 gives v+1; v = 2 gives 0 and carries on (K1-K6, I0/I2/I1a/I1b). - inc on a ready digit: v < 2 gives v+1 (000 → 011 → 001). v = 2 runs the digit as a bell: n increments of the next digit, then the collapse. The digit becomes rest 0, one cell further right, and passes a B> carry to the next digit.   • "ready (0,0) + n × ready (1,0)" is the cell string 000 (011)^n. The bell does not take the target's leading cell, which is the same tape as a rest (0,n) whose last cell is lent to the target. The orbit uses this split 5 times.
  • B> into ready (0,0) sets the leading cell of Y to 1. The re-read in the 6 encodings is a closed form:
  • Y = rest (0,m): ready (1,0), ready (0,m);
  • Y = rest (1,m): ready (1, m+1);
  • Y = ready (0,m), m ≥ 1: rest (1,0), rest (1, m-1);
  • Y blank (top): rest (1,0).
  • Any other Y (rest 2, ready 1 or 2, or a non-top ready (0,0)) leaves a flagged digit (leading cell 1). That is outside the grammar and can halt: ready (2,0) followed by a flagged rest 2 halts at its next inc, through the B> 1 0 path.
  • H1 (field 1's B> into field 2) follows the same table:
  • rest 1 is eaten: the next b is a + 1 + m + 1, then inc(next);
  • ready (1,0) is eaten, then B> passes into the next digit.
  • Field 2 ready (0,·), ready (2,·) or ready (1, m ≥ 1) at H1 is unsupported. With ready (1, m ≥ 1) the next marker turn is not a left visit (a' = a + 2).

Below the top a wave climbs one digit per bell. It dies at the first rest 1 (it eats it), and it cannot pass an older wave. Case (b) needs a new wave to reach an older one.
