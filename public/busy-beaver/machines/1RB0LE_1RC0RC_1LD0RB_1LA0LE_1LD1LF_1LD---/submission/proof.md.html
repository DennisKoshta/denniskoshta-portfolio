1RB0LE_1RC0RC_1LD0RB_1LA0LE_1LD1LF_1LD---: never halts

This machine never halts. After a short start, its run splits into macro steps, and between two macro steps the tape holds a binary counter. Each digit of the counter is made of two blocks of cells: a digit is 0 when two blank cells separate its blocks, and 1 when the blocks touch. The digits are followed by a block of fixed shape on the right.

A carry step adds 1 to the counter. Every digit it touches pays one unit of fuel from its second block. A digit that is set to 1 after spending its last unit merges into the next digit, and the counter loses a digit.

When all digits are 1, the macro step is an overflow instead. It swaps the two halves of every digit, and the counter restarts at 1 with one more digit.

The rules can fail only if a carry reaches a digit 0 that has no fuel left. The proof is an invariant, made of parities and lower bounds, that rules this out at every macro step. Which digits merge, and when, is irregular, but the invariant never needs to know.

Overview

Summary

Here w^n stands for n copies of the word w, and 0^3 for three blank cells.

At the right edge the tape is K(d_1 .. d_m; a, b) (§1). This is a binary counter whose digits are the blocks

(10)^u 00 (01)^v      bit 0
(10)^u (01)^v         bit 1

followed by a block

(10)^a 0^3 (10)^b

Two macro rules move the tape from one such configuration to the next. Each is an exact run composed of 20 window lemmas (§2).

  • M (carry) adds 1 to the counter. Every digit it touches pays one 01 from its v, and the digit flipped 0→1 also gains a 10. A flipped digit with no 01 left merges into the next digit (a drop), and the counter loses a digit.
  • O (overflow), when all bits are 1, swaps the two halves of every digit. The counter restarts at 1 with one more digit.

The proof idea

The rules fail only if a carry reaches a bit-0 digit with no 01 left.

The proof is a per-step invariant (§3):

INV = Common ∧ (A ∨ B ∨ C)

It follows the counter through three phases:

  • A: a cycle with no drops;
  • B: a cycle whose top digit is about to fall;
  • C: the rest of that cycle.

INV is stated through R_j, the number of carries still to come into digit j before the next overflow if no digit above it drops. Its clauses are parities, fuel bounds v_j ≥ R_j + 1 and lower bounds 2^j. They exclude the failure case.

Four arithmetic facts F0–F3 (§3.1) show that M and O keep INV. Which digits drop when is irregular, but the invariant never needs to know.

The run enters INV at step 483 (§4), and progress_nonhalt_cond with P := INV concludes (§5).

1. Family and notation

Tape words are written left to right, and 0^inf is the infinite blank half-tape. B> means the head is in state B on the next cell; §2.1 gives the full head notation.

The configurations K

K(d_1 .. d_m ; a, b) := 0^inf 10 01  d_1 d_2 .. d_m  (10)^a 0^3 (10)^b  B> 0^inf

digit d = (u, beta, v):   (u,0,v) := (10)^u 00 (01)^v        "bit 0"
                          (u,1,v) := (10)^u (01)^v           "bit 1"
  • d_1 is next to the fixed leading 10 01.
  • d_m is next to the right-hand block and is the least significant bit.
  • m ≥ 1.

Canonical form

Every digit has v ≥ 1, and every bit-1 digit has u ≥ 1. Bit-0 digits may have u = 0.

INV states are canonical, because Common (§3) requires it, and M and O map INV states to canonical states (§3.3).

This does not hold outside INV: M turns a bit-1 digit with v = 1 into (u,0,0), which is not canonical. That digit is the precursor of the failure mode (§2.4).

Re-reading (no machine steps)

A bit-1 digit with v = 0 is just (10)^u, so it fuses with what follows:

  • followed by (u',β',v'), it becomes (u+u', β', v');
  • if it is last, it joins the block: a := a + u.

We call this a drop of that digit.

Derived quantities

They are used only in the invariant, not in the rules. All are exact integers.

bits beta_1..beta_m, read as a binary number with beta_m least significant:
U_j  = (beta_1 .. beta_j)_2        V = U_m          R_tot = 2^m - 1 - V
R_j  = 2^j - 1 - U_j               carries that will still touch d_j before the next overflow, if no digit above it drops
R1_j = (R_j + 1 - beta_j) / 2      how many of those set d_j to 1   (R_j ≡ 1 + beta_j mod 2)
ubar_j = u_j + R1_j                vbar_j = v_j - R_j               u_j, v_j as they will be at the next overflow
S = a + R_tot - (2^(m+1) - 2)      T = b - (2V - 1)

§6 gives the same definitions in additive form, with no subtraction or division, as the .v states them.

2.1 Building blocks

Notation. X> means the head is in state X on the next cell, and <X means it is in state X on the previous cell (busycoq's {{X}}> and <{{X}}).

A window lemma w → w' means: started on the cells of w, the machine runs the stated number of steps without reading a cell outside w (the last step may move the head onto a neighbouring cell), and leaves w'. Such a lemma holds in every tape context.

How the lemmas are proved:

  • T1–T14 are exact finite runs (in the .v, each is one es).
  • S1–S6 are proved for one block by a finite run, and induction on n gives every n (in the .v, shift_rule_R/shift_rule_L, and plain induction for S5).

2.4 What the rules do, in words

The digits form a binary counter. Each M is one increment. It flips the trailing 1s to 0 and the last 0 to 1, and every digit it touches pays one 01 from its v. A digit flipping 0→1 also gains a 10.

When all bits are 1, O swaps the two halves of every digit:

  • each (10)^u becomes the (01)^u fuel of a new bit-0 digit;
  • each (01)^v becomes the next digit's (10)^(v-1).

The counter restarts at value 1 with one more digit.

When a digit flipping 0→1 has spent its last 01 (v = 1), it drops: its 10s merge into the next digit, and the counter loses that digit.

The only way the rules can fail is the failure mode: a carry arriving at a bit-0 digit with v = 0, where Lf's first lemma T4 has no 01 to read. That happens if a carry flips a bit-1 digit with v = 1 to 0.

Every macro step is an exact run of at least 32 steps, so every macro step makes progress.

3. Invariant: closure and coverage

The invariant is

INV(D; a, b) := Common ∧ (A ∨ B ∨ C)

The three classes are told apart by the top digit d_1.

  • Common:
  • m ≥ 1, and the tape is canonical (every v_j ≥ 1, every bit-1 u_j ≥ 1);
  • a ≥ 1, b odd, a + R_tot even;
  • S ≥ 0, T ≥ 0;
  • vbar_m ≥ 1 (the LSB never runs out).
  • A (quiet cycle):
  • d_1 = (0,0,v) with v ≥ 2, or d_1 = (1,1,v);
  • for all j, vbar_j ≥ 1 and ubar_j is odd;
  • S ≥ 1.
  • B (top about to fall):
  • d_1 = (0,0,1);
  • for all j, v_j + beta_j is odd ("safe parity");
  • for j ≥ 2, ubar_j ≥ 2^(j-1).
  • C (top has fallen):
  • for all j, v_j + beta_j is odd and ubar_j ≥ 2^j;
  • S ≥ 1, T ≥ 2.

The run cycles A → B → C → A:

  • A is a cycle with no drops.
  • B is the next cycle, whose top digit (0,0,1) falls at its first touch and is absorbed by the digit below.
  • C is the rest of that cycle.

Seen at the overflows, the phases alternate: all u odd (end of A), then all v even (end of C).

3.1 Four arithmetic facts (they follow from the rules alone)

F0 (prefix bound)

0 ≤ U_j ≤ 2^j − 1,   so R_j ≥ 0,   and   R_j ≡ 1 + beta_j (mod 2)

If some bit beta_i = 0 with i ≤ j, then U_j ≤ 2^j − 2, so R_j ≥ 1. With the parity:

  • a bit-0 digit then has R_j odd, so R_j ≥ 1;
  • a bit-1 digit then has R_j even, so R_j ≥ 2.

In an M step every touched digit j ≥ j* has the bit-0 digit j* in its prefix, so F0 applies to all of them.

F1 (touch)

In an M step every digit j ≥ j* is touched. Its prefix value U_j grows by exactly 1, because bits j*..j go from 01..1 to 10..0. So R_j drops by 1:

  • a 1→0 touch: v−1, R1 unchanged;
  • a 0→1 touch: u+1, v−1, R1−1.

So ubar_j and vbar_j are unchanged, and v_j + beta_j keeps its parity. Digits j < j* are untouched and unchanged.

M also gives

a+1,   b+2,   V+1,   R_tot−1

so a + R_tot, S and T are unchanged.

F2 (drop of d_{j}, j < m)

Let H = U_{j*−1}. Digits above j* are unchanged. A digit j > j* moves to index j−1.

Compare its new R', R1' with the values R°, R1° that F1 alone would give:

R° − R' = 2^(j−j*) · (2^(j*−1) − 1 − H) ≥ 0                 (so vbar only grows)
2·R1'   = R1° + 2^(j−j*−1)            for j ≥ j*+2  ⇒  ubar' ≥ ubar/2 + 2^(j−j*−2)
absorber j = j*+1:  u' = u_{j*+1} + u_{j*} + 1,  R1' = R1_{j*}  ⇒  ubar' = u_{j*+1} + ubar_{j*} + 1 ≥ ubar_{j*} + 1
if j* = 1 (H = 0):  R' = R°, R1' = R1°   (a falling top changes nothing below except the indices)

Here ubar_{j*} is the dropper's value before the step.

A drop also changes the global quantities:

  • V' = H·2^(m−j*), so T grows by 2(H+1)2^(m−j*) ≥ 2;
  • S grows by 2^(m−1) + (H+1)2^(m−j*) ≥ 2;
  • R_tot' and R°_tot are both odd, so a + R_tot keeps its parity.

F3 (overflow)

At an all-ones state:

R = R1 = 0,   so   ubar = u,   vbar = v,   V = 2^m − 1   and   R_tot = 0

After O there are m+1 digits and V' = 1:

j = 1:        ubar' = 1                    vbar' = u_1 − 1
2 ≤ j ≤ m:    ubar' = v_{j−1} − 1 + 2^(j−1)   vbar' = u_j − (2^j − 1)
j = m+1:      ubar' = v_m + 2^m − 1        vbar' = a − (2^(m+1) − 2)
a' + R_tot' = b + 1 + 2^(m+1)  (even)      T' = 0      S' = b + 3 − 2^(m+1) = T (the T before O)

3.2 Coverage (a rule always applies)

Take an INV state.

  • Not all bits 1: M applies. It needs v_j ≥ 1 (j ≥ j), u_j ≥ 1 (j > j), a ≥ 0 and b ≥ 0, all in Common. In particular, Common's v_{j*} ≥ 1 is exactly what rules out the failure mode.
  • All bits 1: O applies (m ≥ 1, all u, v ≥ 1, a ≥ 1).

There is no case split beyond M/O.

3.3 Closure (each case)

M keeps Common

  • A digit touched 1→0 has v ≥ 2, so its new v ≥ 1:
  • in A, vbar ≥ 1 gives v ≥ R+1, and F0 gives R ≥ 2, so v ≥ 3;
  • in B/C, v + 1 is odd, so v is even and ≥ 2.
  • The 0→1 digit either keeps v ≥ 1 or drops.
  • The LSB never drops. A 0→1 touch of d_m has R_m ≥ 1 (F0), and vbar_m ≥ 1 then gives v_m ≥ 2. So every drop has j* < m, an absorber exists, and m' = m−1 ≥ 1.
  • The flipped digit gets u+1 ≥ 1.
  • The rest follows from F1/F2.

A, M step → A

  • No drop is possible: the 0→1 digit has R ≥ 1 (F0) and vbar ≥ 1, so v ≥ R+1 ≥ 2.
  • F1 keeps every vbar, ubar and S.
  • A top (0,0,v≥2) may become (1,1,v−1) with v−1 ≥ 1.
  • A top (1,1,v) is never touched by M, because the carry stops at the last 0, which lies below it.

B, M step

j* ≥ 2 → B.

  • Parity is kept by F1.
  • A non-absorber has ubar' ≥ 2^(j−2) + 2^(j−j*−2) ≥ 2^((j−1)−1).
  • The absorber has ubar' ≥ ubar_{j*} + 1 ≥ 2^(j*−1).
  • d_1 is unchanged.

j* = 1 → C. The top (0,0,1) flips with v = 1 and falls.

  • By F2 with H = 0, every other digit keeps its ubar and loses one index, so ubar ≥ 2^(j−1) = 2^(new j).
  • The new d_1 is the absorber: ubar' = u + ubar_top + 1 = u + 2 ≥ 2.
  • S grows by 2^m ≥ 2, so S ≥ 1. T grows by 2^m ≥ 4, so T ≥ 2.

B never reaches all-ones (beta_1 = 0), and B has m ≥ 2 (if m = 1, vbar_1 = 0).

C, M step → C

  • Parity is kept by F1.
  • A non-absorber below a drop has ubar' ≥ ubar/2 ≥ 2^(j−1).
  • The absorber has ubar' ≥ ubar_{j*} + 1 > 2^(j*). This covers j* = 1, a second top fall.
  • S and T only grow.

A, O step → B

At all-ones the top is (1,1,v), so u_1 = 1 and the new top is (0,0,1).

Safe parity:

  • the top: v = 1, bit 0;
  • j ≤ m: v = u_j = ubar_j, odd, bit 0;
  • the LSB: v = a, even because a + R_tot is even with R_tot = 0, bit 1.

Bounds and Common:

  • ubar' = v_{j−1} − 1 + 2^(j−1) ≥ 2^(j−1), and for the LSB v_m + 2^m − 1 ≥ 2^m.
  • S' = T ≥ 0.
  • vbar'_{m+1} = a − 2^(m+1) + 2 ≥ 1, because S ≥ 1 means a ≥ 2^(m+1) − 1.
  • b' = 1, a' = b+3, and every new v ≥ 1.

C, O step → A

  • u_j = ubar_j ≥ 2^j, so the new top is (0,0,u_1) with u_1 ≥ 2.
  • vbar': u_1 − 1 ≥ 1, u_j − 2^j + 1 ≥ 1, and a − 2^(m+1) + 2 ≥ 1 (from S ≥ 1).
  • ubar' is odd: 1, v_{j−1} − 1 + 2^(j−1) and v_m + 2^m − 1 are all odd, because every v is even (from v + 1 odd) and j ≥ 2.
  • S' = T ≥ 2 ≥ 1.

So INV is closed under the macro step, and every INV state has a macro step.

5. Conclusion

  1. The run reaches an INV state (§4).
  2. Every INV state has a rule (§3.2).
  3. Every rule is an exact run of at least 32 steps, composed of the window lemmas of §2.1 (§2.2, §2.3), and every result is again in INV (§3.3).
  4. By the progress theorem the machine never halts.

In the .v this is nonhalt: multistep_nonhalt with init, then progress_nonhalt_cond with P := INV and the closure lemma

step : INV s -> exists s', Kc s -->+ Kc s' /\ INV s'

Why --- (F reading 1) is never used

Every step of the run, after the 483-step initial segment, lies inside one of the 20 window lemmas. F is entered only in T4, T9 and T13, each time by E1, and each time F reads 0 (the transition lists in §2.1).

The initial segment is a finite run that does not halt either.

We claim nothing about tapes outside the family; INV keeps the run inside it.

6. Mapping to busycoq

The template, TM21

  • Family. K is built on the Fixpoint app_ds over a list of digits dg, as TM21's LC0/LC1 over (n, m) pairs, but every digit carries its own bit (d0 u v, d1 u v). The list is least significant digit first. The near block becomes (10)^a 0^3 (10)^b, a fixed right side.
  • Overflow. O is a list re-pairing (Oz/Oout), proved by list induction (RoS, Ozeq, O_spec), following TM21's Ov/Ov_spec. T9 runs on 0inf.
  • Carry.
  • TM21's LIncs_spec is not a per-carry lemma. It takes an all-C0 list to an all-C1 list over a whole counter cycle (k = 2^len − 1 increments, sideRLs on tm' = flip tm with hLR2^^k).
  • TM21 has no per-carry lemma, so the per-carry M lemma here is new.
  • M_spec is Lb by induction over the list of trailing bit-1 digits (LbS, phase 2), then Lf, then Rb by induction over the same list (RbS, phase 3), with an arbitrary left side X.
  • This follows the pattern of LIncs_spec's induction over the pairs: one pair per induction step, joined to the induction hypothesis (by segRLs_sideRLs_concat there, by follow here).
  • Drops are tape equalities only (lpow_add), with no steps: Mdrop_spec is M_spec followed by one.
  • Window lemmas are direct es runs on tm, extended by shift_rule_R/shift_rule_L (S5 by induction), not flip/sideRLs.
  • Macro step and invariant.
  • TM21 iterates a whole-cycle BigStep under LWF, which bounds each pair by 2^(length x) ≤ m.
  • Here drops happen at data-dependent times, so a cycle has no closed form, and no such per-pair bound holds, because digits run out and merge.
  • The macro step is therefore one M, one M with a drop, or one O, and the invariant is the per-step INV of §3: digits carry a bit, and INV has three phases with parity conditions and 2^j bounds.
  • Conclusion. nonhalt uses multistep_nonhalt + progress_nonhalt_cond with P := INV on macro states (ds, a, b), as TM21 does with LWF.

The .v is 694 lines, about 2.25 times TM21's 308.

INV without truncating subtraction

In nat, v − R, S, T and the division in R1 would truncate silently.

Every clause of INV has an additive form:

R_j + U_j + 1 = 2^j
2·R1_j + beta_j = R_j + 1        (both sides exact, because R_j ≥ 0 and R_j ≡ 1 + beta_j)
R_tot + V + 1 = 2^m
vbar_j ≥ 1     ⟺  v_j ≥ R_j + 1
ubar_j odd     ⟺  u_j + R1_j odd
ubar_j ≥ 2^j   ⟺  u_j + R1_j ≥ 2^j
S ≥ 0          ⟺  a + R_tot + 2 ≥ 2^(m+1)
S ≥ 1          ⟺  a + R_tot + 1 ≥ 2^(m+1)
T ≥ 0          ⟺  b + 1 ≥ 2V       ⟺  b + 3 + 2·R_tot ≥ 2^(m+1)
T ≥ 2          ⟺  b + 1 ≥ 2V + 2   ⟺  b + 1 + 2·R_tot ≥ 2^(m+1)

The .v states INV in these forms, with R computed directly and no U_j.

R by recursion

On the LSB-first list:

Rof []        = 0
Rof (d0 :: t) = 2·Rof t + 1
Rof (d1 :: t) = 2·Rof t

On the digits d_j .. d_1 it gives R_j (since R_j = 2·R_{j−1} + 1 − beta_j), and on the whole list it gives R_tot.

Per-digit predicates

Every per-digit condition is a predicate P d r p, where r = Rof of the digits above d and p = how many there are (p = j − 1). AllD P applies it to every digit.

Per digit:

Rd d r = 2r + 1 − beta      = R_j
ub d r = u + r + 1 − beta   = ubar_j

So the per-digit data have no subtraction and no division, and the only power of 2 is the bound in PB.

The classes

  • PA = canonical, v ≥ R + 1 (vbar ≥ 1), ubar odd, and ubar = 1 for the top digit (equivalent to A's condition on d_1, given v ≥ R + 1).
  • PB k = canonical, v + beta odd, ubar ≥ 2^(p+k), and for k = 0 the top is (0,0,1); k = 0 is class B and k = 1 is class C. B's bound is uniform, since the top (0,0,1) has ubar = 1 = 2^0.
  • LSBok is vbar_m ≥ 1.
  • The global clauses are the additive forms above, with S ≥ 1 in A and C and T ≥ 2 in C.

F0 and F1

  • F0 is Rof_bound (R + 1 ≤ 2^len) and Rof_head1/Rof_head0 (the parity of R).
  • F1 is the lemma F1, generic over any per-digit predicate closed under the two touches (PA_touch0/1, PB_touch0/1), with Rof_M, len_M, LSB_M.

F2 without 2^j

Along the chain of digits below the absorber, the old and new R values satisfy

Rel r ro rn := rn + 1 ≤ ro ≤ 2(rn + 1) ∧ (r = 0 → ro = rn + 1)

which is preserved digit by digit (ro' = 2ro, rn' = 2rn + 1). That gives ubar' ≥ ubar/2 with the index one lower, and for a top fall (r = 0) ubar' = ubar (B → C).

Lemmas: Rel, PB_chain, PB_abs, F2chain, F2, LSB_drop.

F3 and the remaining Common facts

F3 is two list inductions, Oz_AB (A → B) and Oz_CA (C → A), using Oz_R:

Rof (Oz t u) + 1 = 2^(len t + 1)

i.e. after O the m re-paired bit-0 digits above the new LSB have R = 2^m − 1.

The remaining Common facts close by lia after rewriting every power to 2 · 2^m' (pow2S).

7. Numerical sanity checks

They are evidence only; the result rests on the .v file alone.

The window lemmas, M, O, the initial segment, the run and the other starts are checked by direct simulation of the transition table. The orbit, inductiveness and F1–F3 checks evaluate the macro rules M/O and INV on abstract states (the 2,842-state subset is also run on the machine).

  • Window lemmas. S1–S6 (one block) and T1–T14 run exactly as stated and never read outside their windows. F is entered only in T4, T9 and T13, and reads 0 each time.
  • M, link by link (§2.2). After every lemma application, the whole tape, head and state equal the stated intermediate configuration; the end is the M result, the total is the step formula, and the leftmost cell read is the first 0 of d_{j*}'s 00.
  • 37,220 instances: an exhaustive box (4 left contexts, u* ≤ 2, v* ≤ 3, k ≤ 3, u_i, v_i ≤ 2, a ≤ 3 including a = 0, b ≤ 2) plus 500 random ones (k ≤ 7, values up to 360).
  • M is also exact on all 256,000 configurations of a second exhaustive box.
  • O, link by link (§2.3).
  • The same checks on 9,975 instances: m ≤ 3 with u_i, v_i ≤ 3, m = 4 with u_i, v_i ≤ 2, a ≤ 3, b ≤ 2, plus 300 random ones (m ≤ 8).
  • O's end result is also exact on 66,336 configurations of an exhaustive box and on 20,000 random ones.
  • Initial segment (§4). From the blank tape: K((3,1,1); 2, 1) at step 142, K((1,1,2); 8, 5) at step 483.
  • The run. From the blank tape to step 2·10^9, the machine passes 26,396 macro boundaries, and each is the K-configuration that M/O predict.
  • INV along the orbit. Along the abstract macro orbit from step 483, INV holds for 1.2·10^6 macro steps (to t ≈ 3.7·10^12 by the step formulas, ending with 16 digits (17 at most), with 52 top falls and 26 drops below the top).
  • INV is inductive.
  • From 750,000 random INV states of all three classes (not only reachable ones; m ≤ 10, values near the thresholds and up to 5000), one macro step is defined, has no LSB drop, and lands in INV; all six transitions A→A, A→B, B→B, B→C, C→A, C→C occur.
  • The same holds for every state in exhaustive boxes up to m = 6 (about 2.5·10^8 states).
  • 2,842 of the random states, run on the machine for the predicted number of steps, give exactly the abstract successor, drops included.
  • F1–F3. The drop closed forms (F2: R, R1, V, S, T after a drop) hold on 20,000 random cases with m = 2..12 and every j*; F1–F3 hold on all 1,470 cases with m ≤ 9.
  • Other starts (§4). Started in B, the table reaches K((1,1,10); 8, 1) at step 732; started in C, K((1,1,9); 8, 1) at step 687; started in D, K((1,1,3); 4, 1) at step 215. All three are class A.
