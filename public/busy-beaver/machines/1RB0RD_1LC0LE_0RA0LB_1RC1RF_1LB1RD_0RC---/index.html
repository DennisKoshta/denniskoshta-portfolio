Mechanism

How it works: 3 notes
  • The tape is runs of 100 blocks joined by markers: one hub marker 0 (W5-5's 0001 defect, mirrored), 101 separators, and a unary counter (101)^c at the right end. HUB(k), exact: the head zigzags left through (101)^c, makes 3^(k+1) short trips at the hub (each moves one 100 block across it), then adds one 101 to the counter; k = number of 101 separators right of the hub.
  • When the hub's right segment b drops below 3^(k+1), the outcome branches on b and the neighbours (99 events to 1e9): the hub becomes a 11 marker, moves, or the head sweeps to the left edge. Once per deep cycle (88 cycles to 1.55e11) the head reaches the left edge; then the counter drains one 101 per long trip while inner hubs work in older segments, which later freeze (58, 106, 1452, 393, 165, 338, 838, 12269, 41798).
  • No halt to 1.55e11. Head windows (radius 4-10) stop changing after 1e7 steps, but plain NGramCPS fails for n = 2-20. Halting needs A or D to meet 111 moving right (A1 = 0RD, D1 = 1RF, F1 = halt); the real tape has 111 only at deep sweeps, where C passes it moving left. 1 of 2,000 random segment tapes halted, and it was malformed.

Proof strategy & riskshow the formal proof will go · 3 risks to watch

Route 1 (NONHALT, preferred): look for a closed regular language on 3-cell words (block CPS / CTL with the gap-token vocabulary); it would not need the residue arithmetic. Route 2: model the exhaustion branches and the deep cycle exactly, then look for a closed residue table. Stop if the exhaustion law is a pseudo-random residue map.

  • ▲Exhaustion branches on b mod 3^(k+1) and re-cuts segments; frozen segment lengths and counter values look irregular (Collatz-type signal). A 111 met by A or D moving right halts, and 111 does occur at deep sweeps.
  • ▲Plain NGramCPS fails for n = 2..20, so any regular invariant must use phase or block structure; it may not exist.
  • ▲Deep cycles get long (2.9e10 steps per cycle at 1.5e11), so long-run evidence grows slowly.
