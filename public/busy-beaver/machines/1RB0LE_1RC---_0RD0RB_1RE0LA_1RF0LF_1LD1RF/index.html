How it works: 3 notes
  • Same table as SOBCv10.v (ours A..F = its D,E,F,A,B,C, mirrored; rules here use its frame, wall on the left; in our frame all is mirrored, Fig 2). Words: A=11111011 (0), B=11101111 (1), C, D, E markers, U=1011, X=0100, wall W=10100100, edge t=11. A hash = one signal from the wall into the words and back.
  • Each hash adds one X to the wall's tally and increments a binary A/B counter; marker words absorb the carries. When all is B and a carry hits the edge, an exception turns the tally into n fresh hashes and leaves C A U^(2n), which takes 2(2^n-1) hashes to drain: exceptions come at tower-like times.
  • Our run: exceptions at n = 0, 0, 28, 3*2^29-2, then n5 ~ 2^(n4+36.7), giving SOBCv10's post_ca state plus a far-end tail U^3 A t. The tail U^(2k+1) y is one digit with levels -1..4 (A, B, E t, D t, A t, B t). Each exception eats the last column's top digit, adds a U pair and sets the level to k+1-L: 3, -1, 4, 0. In exception 9 a decode carry hits B t: a nested exception E' starts, using exception 9's finished calls as its tally. E' (tail U^13 B t) triggers E'' long before its calls run out. E'''s raw pass overflows at U^13 and, without touching anything to its left, becomes a translated cycler (period 919, shift 3).

No infinite round invariant: the tail ends the SOBCv10 rounds at exception 9. The proof is a finite chain: the opening (families (a)-(d)) to SOBCv10's post_ca state, exceptions 6-9 as SOBCv10 rounds with the tail laws T1-T3, the decode trigger in exception 9, the nested exceptions E' and E'' (exact call counts from T1), a local overflow lemma and the translated cycler.   • ▲Exceptions 6-9 at real size are a model (T1-T3 plus SOBCv10's round formulas). The robustness argument (k = 6 always overflows into the cycler) makes the outcome safe, but the formal path needs the exact chain.
