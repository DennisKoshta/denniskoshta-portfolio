1. Notation and family

Tokens. Cell strings 0 = 00, 1 = 10, O = 100, I = 110. Every token ends in a 0 cell. A token word has a unique parse: a 1 followed by an odd run of 0s starts 1, an even run starts O, and 11 starts I. O and I are called markers; as bits, 0, O are 0 and 1, I are 1.

The background block is B = 10110 = 1 I. Its partner is C = 11010 = I 1. A right-counter digit is I (bit 1) or O (bit 0). The right end is T = 11 0^inf.

Family (a "departure").

Dep(w; g; d_1..d_k; G_1..G_k) := 0^inf  w  E>  (1 I)^g  d_1 (1 I)^G_1  d_2 (1 I)^G_2 ... d_k (1 I)^G_k  T
  • w is a token word (the left word), written left to right; w = u t with t its last token. w may be empty (§2, strip).
  • D = d_1..d_k is a little-endian binary counter (d_1 least significant); G_i is the gap after d_i.
  • On cells: 0^inf w E> (10110)^g d_1 (10110)^G_1 ... d_k (10110)^G_k 11 0^inf.
  • Derived quantities: H_0 = g − d_1 and H_i = G_i − d_(i+1) for 1 ≤ i ≤ k (with d_(k+1) := 0). At an absorption, h_1 := H_1.
  • Sweeps to the next absorption: τ := 2g − 1 − d_1 (g after strip). One GEN sweep lowers τ by 1, each strip raises it by 2, and an absorption is exactly a departure with τ = 0 (g = 1, d_1 = 1).

Strip (re-reading, no steps). If w = x I 1, the tail I 1 is a C block. The returning F turns it into B (W5), so Dep(x I 1; g; ..) behaves exactly like Dep(x; g+1; ..) in the next sweep. All rules below are stated for stripped words (w not ending in I 1). A strip can leave the empty word (O 1 →GEN I 1 → ''; ABS1 on O 1 1 gives I 1 → ''). It does so only while raising g to ≥ 2, so the next sweep is a GEN.

2.2 Right sweep (derived)

  • Pass. From x_1 = 1: W1 on x_1, then for each next token W2 (if I) or W1 (if 1). The carried 1 always sits just left of E. The pass rotates the token segment by one: E> x_1 x_2 .. x_m → x_2 .. x_m x_1 E>. On blocks: R1 E> B^n → C^n E>, 17n steps (n uses of W1+W2).
  • Turn. At the first digit O: W3. At T: W4.
  • Return. F re-pairs from the right: W5 on each I 1, W6 on each unpaired I (a carried digit). On blocks: R2 C^n <F → <F B^n, 9n steps.
  • Composed (check B, exact output and step count on random parameters):

2.3 Left turnaround L (checked, not derived)

L. Let w be a stripped word in the domain: the empty word, or first token not 0, last token 0 or 1, and markers pairwise ≥ 3 tokens apart. Let ρ be any right context whose first two cells are 1, 0, i.e. one that starts with a 1 token or an O token. Then:

0^inf w <F ρ   -->   0^inf inc_L(w) E> ρ
  • The head reads no cell right of w, except on an I-crossing with t = 0 (tail I 1^k 0). There it reads exactly the two cells 1, 0 of ρ and restores them.
  • In GEN, ρ = (1 I)^g ... (g ≥ 1). Inside ABS, ρ starts with O, the cleared d_1 (§2.4). Both satisfy the precondition.
  • The precondition is sharp. With an I (cells 110) right of w, every one of the 1,735 t = 0 I-crossing words of length ≤ 9 comes out differently from inc_L.
  • Check C: all 13,184 domain words of length ≤ 9, the empty word included, give exactly inc_L(w) with three contexts ρ (1 I, O 1 I, 1 O). Outside the domain (adjacent markers, I 1 I) the machine differs on 17,066 of 101,504 words.
  • A random review run (8,000 words of up to 60 tokens with up to 11 markers, at spacing 3 and at spacing 4) was also all exact.

Mechanism (from traces): - start. F0 0LA puts A on the bit cell of t. A short gadget restores t. It leaves A on the bit cell of the token left of t, with that token's 0 cell holding a temporary 1. The gadget takes 7 steps for t = 1 (F0 A1 C0 D0 C0 D1 B0) and 9 for t = 0 (F0 A0 B0 A1 C0 D0 C0 D1 B0). - carry. On a 1 token, A runs a 6-step loop (A1 C0 D0 C0 D1 B0) and moves one token left; the passed token becomes 11. - stop. At 0: A0 1RB, and the token becomes 1. At O: it becomes I. At the left edge: a new 1 token. - return. E and B run right (E1 0RB, B1 0RE), clearing each 11 to 00. E stops on the first cell after w. - I-crossing. At I, C1 1LF creates F on the last cell of the token left of the I (a 0), and the turnaround restarts on x. Then the return writes O 0^(k−1) t 1.

Steps: 8k + 13 for marker-free words, where k is the number of trailing 1s of u (check C). Words with markers: no formula is derived yet.

Owed (major). The start/carry/stop/return/crossing pieces as window lemmas, with an induction on the carry length and a recursion on the I-crossing. Until then, L is a checked statement, not a lemma.

  • ABS1, ABS0, ABSO1 (g = 1, d_1 = 1): R4/R5 with a = 1 returns F with O (the cleared d_1) directly right of w. Then come L (context O), W3 (E> O → <F I), a second L, and an absorption tail in which E departs into I. Only the first part is derived. The whole rule is checked:

3. Invariant and coverage

INV(w, g, D, G) := INV-R ∧ P_k ∧ INV-W ∧ Safe ∧ X, where X is the missing clause that would make Safe inductive (§3.4). Without X this is not an invariant.

3.1 INV-R and P_k (right part), proved

INV-R: - k ≥ 1 and g ≥ 1; - G_k = 0; - H_i ≥ 0 for 1 ≤ i ≤ k; - H_1 ≥ H_2 ≥ … ≥ H_k; - H_i ≥ 1 for 1 ≤ i ≤ k−3.

P_k (replaces O1): k ≥ 2, or (k = 1, D = [1], and (g ≥ 2 or the last token of w is 1)).

  • H1 (one inc, carry stopping at digit j):
  • H_0 drops by 1 iff j = 1 (d_1 set). Otherwise g−1 and d_1: 1→0 cancel.
  • For 1 ≤ i ≤ j−2, digits i and i+1 both shift, so G_i is unchanged; d_(i+1) goes 1→0, so H_i gains 1.
  • H_(j−1): G_(j−1)+1 and d_j: 0→1 cancel.
  • Above j nothing changes.
  • Overflow clears every digit, so H_i gains 1 for i ≤ k−1. The new digits have H_k = H_(k+1) = H_(k+2) = 0.

So inc never lowers any H_i (i ≥ 1). Equivalently, H_i counts the clears of digit i+1. - H2. For i ≥ 1, H_i − H_(i+1) never decreases: every clear of digit i+2 is also a clear of digit i+1. At overflow the new tail is …, H_(k−1)+1 ≥ 1 > 0 = H_k = H_(k+1) = H_(k+2). So the chain H_1 ≥ … ≥ H_k is kept. - H3. The last gap never changes while it is last, and overflow appends gaps 0 and 0, so G_k = 0. g drops only on a carry through d_1, which needs g ≥ 2 in GEN, so GEN keeps g ≥ 1. Strip only raises g. - Absorption (ABS1 c = 2, ABS0 c = 1). The new right part is inc(G_1 + c, d_2.., G_2..), so indices shift by one: new H_i = old H_(i+1), then H1 applies. The H clauses are kept. For g: - k ≥ 2: new g = G_1 + c − d_2 = h_1 + c ≥ 1 (if d_2 = 1 the inc carries and g = G_1 + c − 1; if d_2 = 0 it sets d_1 and g = G_1 + c). - k = 1: by P_k, D = [1] and (g ≥ 2, which is not an absorption, or t = 1). With t = 1 the rule is ABS1 or ABSO1. ABS1 gives inc(G_1 + 2 = 2, [], []) = (g = 1, D = 00, G = 00), so k = 2. ABSO1 never lowers k. - So ABS0 never runs with k = 1. That case would really give g = 0, a configuration outside the family; the review checked that the model and the machine agree there, 259 of 259. - P_k is kept (one step): - k only drops at an absorption, by one. - An absorption at k = 2 with d_2 = 0 gives k = 1, D = [1] and g = G_1 + c (G_2 = 0). ABS1 has c = 2, so g ≥ 2. ABS0 outputs a word ending in 1 1. Either way P_k holds. - An absorption at k = 2 with d_2 = 1 overflows to k = 3. - From k = 1, D = [1]: a GEN overflows to k = 3. An absorption has t = 1 (above), so it is ABS1 (k → 2) or ABSO1 (k → 3). - Strip only raises g. - At the start k = 2. - Check E: exhaustive one-step check on all words of ≤ 7 tokens and right parts with k ≤ 4, G_i ≤ 4, g ≤ 4 satisfying INV-R: 2,358,665 transitions at spacing ≥ 4 and 3,047,785 at spacing ≥ 3, 0 failures. ABS0? cases are left to Safe. - H4 (interval). From an absorption with (h_1, d_2, c), the next absorption comes after exactly Δ = 2(h_1 + c) − 1 + d_2 + 2s sweeps, where s is the number of strips in the interval. This follows from τ (§1): after the absorption τ = 2(h_1 + c) − 2 + d_2, and each strip adds 2. The earlier statement without 2s was wrong whenever a strip occurs; strips are common (check F: 438 of 438 intervals match the corrected formula). - H5. H_i ≥ 1 for i ≤ k−3 is kept: - inc only raises H; - overflow raises every old H_i (i ≤ k−1) by 1 while k grows by 2; - absorption shifts indices down by 1 while k drops by 1. - T (transient tails). After ABS1 the next two sweeps are GEN: - d_2 = 0: the new state is (G_1+2, d_1 = 1) → GEN gives (G_1+1, d_1 = 0) → GEN. - d_2 = 1: G_1 ≥ 1 by H_1 ≥ 0. The new state is (G_1+1, 0) → GEN gives (G_1+1 ≥ 2, 1) → GEN.

This removes the transient tails x I 0, x O 1 and x I 1 that ABS1 can leave before any absorption sees them: … O 1 1 → … I 1 (strip); … I 0 1 → x' O 1 → x' I 1 (strip); … I 1 1 → x' I 0 → x'' O 1 → x'' I 1 (strip). - Removed: the old claims "h_1 ≥ 2 implies next h_1 ≥ 1" and the next-h_1 formula. Both are false when an overflow comes before the next absorption. Counterexample, checked on the machine: Dep(1010; 1; 10; 2,0), ABS0, then the next absorption is Dep(101I01; 1; 110; 1,0,0), with h_1 = 0 and k = 3. P_k makes them unnecessary.

3.2 INV-W (word shape)

INV-W: w is empty (only right after a strip, with g ≥ 2), or: - the first token is not 0; - the last token is 0 or 1; - markers are pairwise ≥ 3 tokens apart. This was 4 before the review. The rules are exact at spacing 3 (check D: L exhaustively, the departure rules on random words with markers exactly 3 apart). The trajectory keeps ≥ 4, so the weaker clause loses nothing and removes the spacing-3 unsafe values from Safe.

This is L's domain (§2.3). - GEN. inc_L never moves a marker: O→I, I→O and the I-crossing all rewrite in place. The left-edge case shifts all indices uniformly. The I-crossing keeps the marker at its index and ends the word in 1. The left edge always gets a 1 (inc_L('') = 1 0). A trailing I 1 is stripped; by the spacing, the token before it is not a marker. Kept. - ABS1. It applies when the last token is 1 and the tail is not O 1 (that is ABSO1, and I 1 was stripped). Two inc_L keep the markers in place. Dropping the last token leaves the x I 0/x O 1/x I 1 transients of T, which are not absorption inputs, or a word ending in 0/1. w = 1 gives 1 0. Kept, with T. - ABSO1. inc_L(x) is a GEN-type step on x. Kept. - ABS0 creates a marker O. In the case v 0 1^k it sits at position 3 from the right. In the case v 0 1^k 0 it replaces the 0 in front of the 1-run. ABS0 keeps INV-W only if that 0 exists (not a marker) and no other marker is within 2 tokens of the new one. This is part of Safe.

3.3 Safe (formerly O2): a predicate on one configuration

Between absorptions only GEN acts, and it touches w only through inc_L (and strip, which adds 2 to τ). So the next absorption is determined by (w, τ) alone:

Safe(w, τ):  repeat { strip w (τ += 2 per strip); if τ = 0 stop; w := inc_L(w); τ := τ − 1 }
             at the stop: last token 1, or (last token 0 and abs0(w) is defined and leaves markers >= 3 apart)

Safe(w, 2g − 1 − d_1) is a decidable property of a single configuration. With INV-R, P_k and INV-W it guarantees that the next absorption has a rule and that its image again satisfies INV-R, P_k and INV-W. On the trajectory it holds at every interval (check F: 440 of 440 to 1e8; with spacing ≥ 4 also 440 of 440).

It is not inductive. After an absorption the next interval has length Δ' = 2(h_1 + c) − 1 + d_2 + 2s, and Safe for it depends on the low bits of the new word at a time set by the right counter. INV-R, P_k, INV-W and Safe together do not exclude a failure. The review gives configurations satisfying all of them that halt, for example Dep(1O100; 1; 11; 2,0) (halts at step 940) and Dep(1001001O1100; 1; 10; 2,0) (step 1,436). Of 323 random starts failing only the O2 clause, 85 halted within 2e6 steps.

What is proved about markers: - Case-(i) markers (tail O 1 1) cycle O11 → I01 → I11 → x' O11 with t = 1 until the next absorption, which is therefore ABS1. Then T removes them within two sweeps. - Crossing period. A marker at position p from the right (t = position 1) has a (p−2)-bit counter below it. A full cycle O → I → crossing takes 2^(p−1) − 1 sweeps once t = 1, and any I-crossing sets t = 1. So a marker with 2^(p−1) − 1 < Δ cannot be present at an absorption with t = 0. In practice only leading markers (index 0) reach a t = 0 absorption. - Unsafe values. For a word M s t with a leading marker M and an m-bit segment s (u = M s), ABS0 is unsafe exactly when: - s = 1^m or 1^(m−1) 0: abs0 is undefined; - s = 0 1^(m−2) 0 or b 0 1^(m−3) 0: the new O lands 1 or 2 tokens from M.

As fractions of 2^m these are ≈ 1, 1/2 and 1/4, 3/4 (spacing 4 would add 1/8, 3/8, 5/8, 7/8).

3.4 Why Safe is open, and what the data say

Mechanism of the dangerous events (model, scripts/review/absmodel.c): - A leading O is born when ABS0 meets u = 1^(m−1) 0 (u = 2^m − 2). The left edge then acts as a 0 and the word becomes O 0^m 1 1. - Its segment then counts the sweeps. The first t = 0 absorption after the birth (when the birth is followed by one ABS1 with even Δ) sees a segment of Δ_1/2 + Δ_2 ≈ 1.5 Δ, compared with the maximum 2^m − 1 = u_c + 1. So the margin is about u_c − 1.5 Δ. - At marker-free ABS0 events the ratio u/Δ_next stays in a narrow band: [1.6060, 2.0006] for every Δ ≥ 2^9, and [1.6067, 2.0000] for Δ ≥ 2^15 (1e10 sweeps). Case-(ii) events (u even) reach ratios above 1.95 1,205 times to 1e9 sweeps. - Leading-O births to 1e10 sweeps, as (sweep, u_c/Δ_next): (340, 1.667), (400,022, 1.620), (1,332,801, 1.775), (6,315,092, 1.630), (19,421,581, 1.859), (73,470,546, 1.912), (1,118,233,557, 1.960), (5,112,107,104, 1.833). The birth at ratio 1.960 is close to the 3/4-point edge (2.0), but no t = 0 absorption saw that marker.

Margins. The only ABS0 events with a marker present to 1e10 sweeps (five) follow births above. Each has a leading O, and the margins are in sweeps to the nearest unsafe word, forward/back, spacing ≥ 3:

But: - the 3/4 point corresponds to u_c ≈ 2.0 Δ, which is the top edge of the band; - later t = 0 absorptions in the same leading-marker episode see segments ≈ (2 − 2^(−j)) Δ (one more halving per intervening ABS1; heuristic, not checked event by event), and 1.75, 1.875, … lie inside the band.

So safety needs u_c to avoid a set of O(1)-wide targets at the roughly one birth per doubling of Δ. No structural reason for this is known. The trajectory survives because births are rare and each hits a target with probability about c/Δ.

5. Conclusion

5.1 What would finish the proof

  1. A clause X such that INV-R ∧ P_k ∧ INV-W ∧ Safe ∧ X is preserved by every rule. This is the fatal gap. The search should start from §3.4: a quantitative relation between the left counter u and the next intervals Δ, Δ', … at each leading-marker birth, strong enough to keep every t = 0 absorption of the episode off the unsafe values.

F1 is the only ---. F is created only by C1 (1LF): in the turns W3/W4, in L's I-crossing, and in the ABS tails. In W3–W6 and R2, F only moves onto the last cell of a token, which is 0. 5.3 Cryptid assessment (protocol triggered; flagged for Dennis)

The old "not a cryptid" claim is withdrawn. The rules themselves are counter increments and fixed rewrites. The halting question, however, is whether a deterministic coupled system avoids a sparse target set: - the left counter (+1 per sweep, halved at ABS1, rewritten by ABS0); - the right counter, whose absorption times Δ ≈ 2h_1 grow like √(sweeps).

Safety is decided by the low bits of u at absorption times. The smallest margin observed is 2 sweeps, at h_1 = 8. Later margins (4 events up to h_1 = 35,748) are 7–13 % of Δ, but no structural lower bound is known.

Heuristic: about one leading-O birth per doubling of Δ, each failing with probability O(1/Δ), so the expected number of failures beyond the simulated range is O(Σ 2^−m), which is tiny. The machine is therefore "probviously" non-halting, but the argument is probabilistic. Per CLAUDE.md the rules are written out (§2, executable in scripts/review/absmodel.c and scripts/deepen/model3.py) and the machine is flagged; stop or continue is Dennis's call. A Rocq rule reduction in the AntiHydra style (the formal half of the protocol) has not been written.
