1. Family and notation

K(d_1 .. d_m ; a, b) := 0^inf <C (01)^b 000 (01)^a  d~_m .. d~_2 d~_1  1001 0^inf

digit d = (u, beta, v):   d~(u,0,v) := (10)^v 00 (01)^u        "bit 0"
                          d~(u,1,v) := (10)^v (01)^u           "bit 1"
  • Head notation. X> w means the head is in state X on the first cell of w. w <X means it is in state X on the last cell of w. So 0^inf <C (01)^b has the head on the blank cell just left of the block. This is busycoq's {{X}}> / <{{X}}, and the same convention as #11's proof.md.
  • d_1 is next to the fixed 1001 and is the most significant bit. d_m is next to the block and is the least significant bit. m ≥ 1, except at step 82 of the init (§4).
  • The triple (u, beta, v) means exactly what it means in #11: u counts the 01s on the marker side, and v counts the 10s on the block side. Only the tape picture is mirrored. So the rules and the invariant, stated on triples, are #11's.
  • Canonical form. Every digit has v ≥ 1, and every bit-1 digit has u ≥ 1. Bit-0 digits may have u = 0. INV states are canonical, because Common (§3) requires it, and M and O map INV states to canonical states (§3.3). Outside INV, M can make (u,0,0), the precursor of the failure mode (§2.4).
  • Re-reading (no machine steps). A bit-1 digit with v = 0 is just (01)^u, so it fuses with what lies to its left. If the digit to its left is (u',β',v'), the two read as (u'+u, β', v'). If it is the least significant digit, it joins the block: a := a + u. We call this a drop of that digit.

Derived quantities. They are used only in the invariant, not in the rules, and all are exact integers.

bits beta_1..beta_m, read as a binary number with beta_m least significant:
U_j  = (beta_1 .. beta_j)_2        V = U_m          R_tot = 2^m - 1 - V
R_j  = 2^j - 1 - U_j               carries that will still touch d_j before the next overflow, if no digit above it drops

2.4 What the rules do, in words

The digits form a binary counter, and each M is one increment. - M flips the trailing 1s to 0 and the last 0 to 1. Every digit it touches pays one 10 from its v, and a digit flipping 0→1 also gains a 01. - When all bits are 1, O swaps the two halves of every digit. Each (01)^u becomes the (10)^u fuel of a new bit-0 digit, and each (10)^v becomes the next digit's (01)^(v−1). The counter restarts at value 1 with one more digit. - When a digit flipping 0→1 has spent its last 10 (v = 1), it drops: its 01s merge into the next digit, and the counter loses that digit.

The only way the rules can fail is the failure mode: a carry reaches a bit-0 digit with v = 0, where Lf's first lemma T4 has no 10 to read. That happens if a carry flips a bit-1 digit with v = 1 to 0. Every macro step is an exact run of at least 32 steps, so every macro step makes progress.

3. Invariant: closure and coverage

This section is #11's proof.md §3, unchanged. It is stated on the triples (u, beta, v) and the numbers a, b, and it uses only the rule shapes of §2, which are the same as #11's. The Rocq form of this section (#11's .v, INV and step) has been checked by Rocq for #11. It has also been checked verbatim against this machine's K, with the three rule specs as hypotheses (rocq/abstract_check.v, §6).

INV(D; a, b) := Common ∧ (A ∨ B ∨ C). The three classes are told apart by the top digit d_1.

  • Common:
  • m ≥ 1, and the tape is canonical (every v_j ≥ 1, every bit-1 u_j ≥ 1);
  • a ≥ 1, b odd, a + R_tot even;
  • S ≥ 0, T ≥ 0;
  • vbar_m ≥ 1 (the LSB never runs out).
  • A (quiet cycle):
  • d_1 = (0,0,v) with v ≥ 2, or d_1 = (1,1,v);
  • for all j, vbar_j ≥ 1 and ubar_j is odd;
  • S ≥ 1.
  • B (top about to fall):
  • d_1 = (0,0,1);
  • for all j, v_j + beta_j is odd ("safe parity");
  • for j ≥ 2, ubar_j ≥ 2^(j-1).
  • C (top has fallen):
  • for all j, v_j + beta_j is odd and ubar_j ≥ 2^j;
  • S ≥ 1, T ≥ 2.

The run cycles A → B → C → A. - A is a cycle with no drops. - B is the next cycle. Its top digit (0,0,1) falls at its first touch and is absorbed by the digit below. - C is the rest of that cycle.

Seen at the overflows, the phases alternate: all u odd (end of A), then all v even (end of C).

3.1 Four arithmetic facts (they follow from the rules alone)

F0 (prefix bound). 0 ≤ U_j ≤ 2^j − 1, so R_j ≥ 0, and R_j ≡ 1 + beta_j (mod 2). If some bit beta_i = 0 with i ≤ j, then U_j ≤ 2^j − 2, so R_j ≥ 1. With the parity: - a bit-0 digit then has R_j odd, so R_j ≥ 1; - a bit-1 digit then has R_j even, so R_j ≥ 2.

In an M step every touched digit j ≥ j* has the bit-0 digit j* in its prefix, so F0 applies to all of them.

F1 (touch). In an M step every digit j ≥ j* is touched. Its prefix value U_j grows by exactly 1, because bits j*..j go from 01..1 to 10..0, so R_j drops by 1. - A 1→0 touch: v−1, R1 unchanged. - A 0→1 touch: u+1, v−1, R1−1.

So ubar_j and vbar_j are unchanged, and v_j + beta_j keeps its parity. Digits j < j* are untouched and unchanged. M also gives a+1, b+2, V+1 and R_tot−1, so a + R_tot, S and T are unchanged.

F2 (drop of d_{j}, j < m). Let H = U_{j*−1}. Digits above j* are unchanged. A digit j > j* moves to index j−1. Compare its new R', R1' with the values R°, R1° that F1 alone would give:

R° − R' = 2^(j−j*) · (2^(j*−1) − 1 − H) ≥ 0                 (so vbar only grows)
2·R1'   = R1° + 2^(j−j*−1)            for j ≥ j*+2  ⇒  ubar' ≥ ubar/2 + 2^(j−j*−2)
absorber j = j*+1:  u' = u_{j*+1} + u_{j*} + 1,  R1' = R1_{j*}  ⇒  ubar' = u_{j*+1} + ubar_{j*} + 1 ≥ ubar_{j*} + 1
if j* = 1 (H = 0):  R' = R°, R1' = R1°   (a falling top changes nothing below except the indices)

Here ubar_{j*} is the dropper's value before the step. A drop also changes the global quantities: - V' = H·2^(m−j*), so T grows by 2(H+1)2^(m−j*) ≥ 2; - S grows by 2^(m−1) + (H+1)2^(m−j*) ≥ 2; - R_tot' and R°_tot are both odd, so a + R_tot keeps its parity.

F3 (overflow). At an all-ones state, R = R1 = 0, so ubar = u, vbar = v, V = 2^m − 1 and R_tot = 0. After O there are m+1 digits and V' = 1:

j = 1:        ubar' = 1                    vbar' = u_1 − 1
2 ≤ j ≤ m:    ubar' = v_{j−1} − 1 + 2^(j−1)   vbar' = u_j − (2^j − 1)
j = m+1:      ubar' = v_m + 2^m − 1        vbar' = a − (2^(m+1) − 2)
a' + R_tot' = b + 1 + 2^(m+1)  (even)      T' = 0      S' = b + 3 − 2^(m+1) = T (the T before O)

3.2 Coverage (a rule always applies)

Take an INV state. - Not all bits 1: M applies. It needs v_j ≥ 1 (j ≥ j), u_j ≥ 1 (j > j), a ≥ 0 and b ≥ 0, all in Common. In particular, Common's v_{j*} ≥ 1 is exactly what rules out the failure mode. - All bits 1: O applies (m ≥ 1, all u, v ≥ 1, a ≥ 1).

There is no case split beyond M/O.

3.3 Closure (each case)

M keeps Common. - A digit touched 1→0 has v ≥ 2, so its new v ≥ 1. In A, vbar ≥ 1 gives v ≥ R+1, and F0 gives R ≥ 2, so v ≥ 3. In B/C, v + 1 is odd, so v is even and ≥ 2. - The 0→1 digit either keeps v ≥ 1 or drops. - The LSB never drops. A 0→1 touch of d_m has R_m ≥ 1 (F0), and vbar_m ≥ 1 then gives v_m ≥ 2. So every drop has j* < m, an absorber exists, and m' = m−1 ≥ 1. - The flipped digit gets u+1 ≥ 1. - The rest follows from F1/F2.

A, M step → A. No drop is possible: the 0→1 digit has R ≥ 1 (F0) and vbar ≥ 1, so v ≥ R+1 ≥ 2. F1 keeps every vbar, ubar and S. A top (0,0,v≥2) may become (1,1,v−1) with v−1 ≥ 1. A top (1,1,v) is never touched by M, because the carry stops at the last 0, which lies below it.

B, M step. - j ≥ 2 → B. - Parity is kept by F1. - A non-absorber has ubar' ≥ 2^(j−2) + 2^(j−j*−2) ≥ 2^((j−1)−1). - The absorber has ubar' ≥ ubar_{j*} + 1 ≥ 2^(j*−1). - d_1 is unchanged. - j = 1 → C. The top (0,0,1) flips with v = 1 and falls. - By F2 with H = 0, every other digit keeps its ubar and loses one index, so ubar ≥ 2^(j−1) = 2^(new j). - The new d_1 is the absorber: ubar' = u + ubar_top + 1 = u + 2 ≥ 2. - S grows by 2^m ≥ 2, so S ≥ 1. T grows by 2^m ≥ 4, so T ≥ 2. - B never reaches all-ones (beta_1 = 0), and B has m ≥ 2 (if m = 1, vbar_1 = 0).

C, M step → C. - Parity is kept by F1. - A non-absorber below a drop has ubar' ≥ ubar/2 ≥ 2^(j−1). - The absorber has ubar' ≥ ubar_{j*} + 1 > 2^(j*). This covers j* = 1, a second top fall. - S and T only grow.

A, O step → B. At all-ones the top is (1,1,v), so u_1 = 1 and the new top is (0,0,1). Safe parity: - the top: v = 1, bit 0; - j ≤ m: v = u_j = ubar_j, odd, bit 0; - the LSB: v = a, even because a + R_tot is even with R_tot = 0, bit 1.

Bounds and Common: - ubar' = v_{j−1} − 1 + 2^(j−1) ≥ 2^(j−1), and for the LSB v_m + 2^m − 1 ≥ 2^m. - S' = T ≥ 0. - vbar'_{m+1} = a − 2^(m+1) + 2 ≥ 1, because S ≥ 1 means a ≥ 2^(m+1) − 1. - b' = 1, a' = b+3, and every new v ≥ 1.

C, O step → A. - u_j = ubar_j ≥ 2^j, so the new top is (0,0,u_1) with u_1 ≥ 2. - vbar': u_1 − 1 ≥ 1, u_j − 2^j + 1 ≥ 1, and a − 2^(m+1) + 2 ≥ 1 (from S ≥ 1). - ubar' is odd: 1, v_{j−1} − 1 + 2^(j−1) and v_m + 2^m − 1 are all odd, because every v is even (from v + 1 odd) and j ≥ 2. - S' = T ≥ 2 ≥ 1.

So INV is closed under the macro step, and every INV state has a macro step. Checks 6–9 test this on this machine's orbit and on random INV states (§7).
