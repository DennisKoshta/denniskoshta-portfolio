What the eat is. It is not a separate rebuild. The marker keeps walking right through the counter, one bounce at a time, and each bounce increments the frames to the right of the marker's current S-run. So while the marker crosses frame j, frame j+1 is the low digit. The phase ends at the first frame that has digit 2 when the marker reaches it, or at the counter's end. g and the new counter don't depend on N because the walk only reads the counter.

What k_align counts (traced on the worst phases, 3535 and 1497): - Widths add up. The walk merges frames. Each # frame (D) of the next counter is the merger of one crossed d=3 frame and the d=1 frames after it, and its width is the sum of their widths. The d=1 frames before the first d=3 go into the bell: M' = M + 1 + (sum of w/2 over them). - The chain. Each junction in the next counter is resolved by a J-rule sub-phase. That sub-phase continues the old walk past a d=2 stop, with e-1 incs to the rest instead of e. The chain ends after the last d=2 digit of the post-inc counter: the digits above it are 1, so the last sub-walk runs to the top (T) and its output is aligned. - Cost. So k_align is the sum, over the chain's heads, of the 1-2 carries each head needs at its frame position q, each carry costing about 3^q incs. - Phase 3535: heads at positions 4, 6, 7, 7 and 8; k_align = 11,097 with M = 30,990. - Phase 1497: heads at 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6 and 7; k_align = 4,293. - After the chain. Once the chain has resolved, every digit of the aligned counter is 1. So the next post-inc counter is the base-3 expansion of x = M-1-k_align, written on that counter's frame widths.
