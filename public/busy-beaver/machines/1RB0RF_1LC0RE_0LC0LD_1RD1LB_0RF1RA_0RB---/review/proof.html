Summary. The configuration at each sweep boundary is S(r) = 0^inf 11 D> <r> 0^inf, where r is a word over three 3-cell digits. TM7's proof carries over with three changes:

  1. Where TM7's sweep enters an L digit, ours first runs one extra "+1 pass" over everything to the right. This adds an Inc premise to two clauses of TM7's RIncs.
  2. RIncs gets two new end clauses, Z0 and Z1, that use zero passes. They describe no new machine behaviour (each is an identity between two ways of writing the same tape), but without them the sweep map's domain is not closed under the sweep map (§3.3).
  3. TM7's domain lemma RIncs_nxt gets an extra parameter j, the number of pending +1 passes. Its condition Inv reduces to TM7's when j = 0.

1. Families and notation #

  • Digits: L = 110, 0 = 000, 1 = 100 (cells left to right). For a word r over {L,0,1}, <r> is its cells in order. A word followed by 0^inf denotes the same tape with or without trailing 0 digits.
  • Counter value: inc(r), also written r+1, adds 1 to the little-endian binary number formed by the 0/1 digits of r. L digits are skipped; a carry out of the last digit appends a 1 digit.
  • Family: S(r) := 0^inf 11 D> <r> 0^inf (busycoq S' r), for r in the domain P of §3.3.
  • Passes (busycoq sideRLs). A pass runs over a right part X and never reads a cell left of where it exits, so it holds for every left part:
    • h: B> X ->+ <C X'. The head exits one cell left of where it entered, and X' starts at the same cell as X.
    • h': 1 D> X ->+ <C X'. The 1 belongs to the pass, so X' starts at the cell that held the 1.
  • Inc(r, r'): the pass [h] turns <r> into <r'>.
  • R(tp, k, r, r') (busycoq RIncs):
    • R(hx, k, r, r'): the passes [h', h^k] turn <r> into <r'>.
    • R(h1, k, r, r'): the passes [h^k] turn 1 <r> into <r'>, where the extra 1 cell comes first. So hx is "the eater pass plus k queued passes", and h1 is "k passes draining over a stray 1".

In words:

  • Eater (hx). The eater turns every digit it crosses into L. It gains one pass at each L or 0, and at each L the rest of the word gets +1 (X1, NEW). It switches to draining at the first 1 (X3).
  • Drain (h1, k passes). Each 1 absorbs one pass and becomes L (C2). Each 0 keeps one bit of the count and halves it (C3, C4). At the right end the count is written in bijective binary (R0–R2). An L restarts the eater with the same count and adds +1 to the rest (C1, NEW; this is the "drain-enters-L restart"). With 0 passes left, the drain can only be at the last digit, and it leaves the stray 1 in place (Z0, Z1, NEW).

Worked example. This is the start of the Checkpoint A picture pair, S(L^4 1L1), and it is the extra 1 Dennis noticed there (checked in §7).

  • First eat. Both machines eat the 4 L's (k = 4) and stop at the 1. Ours added +1 at each L, so its rest is 1L1 + 4 = 1L11, while TM7's is still 1L1. The extra 1 digit on the right is the extra bar after the first eat.
  • Drain. The first drain pass enters the L.
    • TM7 restarts the eater on 1.
    • Ours first runs +1 pass number 5: 11 + 1 = 001. The extra 1 is not ignored: the carry turns both 1s into 0s and appends a new 1.
  • Second eat. Ours also eats the two new 0s (k = 6). TM7 stops at once (k = 4).
  • Right end. The count is written in bijective binary: 6 gives 111, 4 gives 101.
  • Result. Ours reaches S(L^9 111) and TM7 reaches S(L^7 101). Ours has two more L's on the left, and the right end differs.

3. The argument #

3.1 Every clause is an exact pass-level transition (RIncs_spec), Rocq-checked #

Claim: R(hx,k,r,r') implies sideRLs ([h'] ++ h^k) <r> <r'>, and R(h1,k,r,r') implies sideRLs (h^k) (1 <r>) <r'>. Proof by induction on R, one clause at a time, as in TM7.

This section compiles. check_proof.py --rocq states Inc and all 12 clauses of R for the real machine string, then proves Inc_spec, seg_hx_ld, seg_h1_ld and RIncs_spec. Print Assumptions is closed. The two segment facts were first probed in probe_segments_2.v.

  • X2, X3, C2, C3, C4, R0, R1, R2. Each clause's run never fires A1, the one transition where the two machines differ (§7 counts A1 firings). So the run is the same as TM7's, and TM7's tactic scripts for these 8 cases (cat3, tr1/tr2, wal, am) are reused verbatim.
  • X1 (eater enters L).
    • TM7: the eater reaches A1 at the middle cell of the 110 in 4 steps (D1, B1, E1, A1). TM7's A1 = 0RC writes 0 and steps right onto the last cell of the 110 in state C. C0 = 0LC then turns left.
    • Ours: A1 = 0RF writes the same 0 and steps onto the same cell in state F. F0 = 0RB then puts B on the first cell of X. That is one h pass over X: B> <X> ->(6j+1) <C <X+1>. It returns with C on the last cell of the 110, which is exactly where TM7 is one step after its A1. From then on the two runs agree.
    • So TM7's segment fact for the L digit, segRLs [h'] [h', h] 110 110, becomes segRLs [h'] [h, h', h] 110 110, followed by the wall for h^k (Rocq: seg_hx_ld). The passes reaching the rest are [h] ++ ([h'] ++ h^(k+1)). That is Inc(r, r1) (Inc_spec below), followed by R(hx, k+1, r1, r') (induction hypothesis), joined by sideRLs_trans.
  • C1 (drain enters L). The same argument. The first pass of h^(k+1) meets 1 110 and reaches A1 in 3 steps (B1, E1, A1). TM7's segment fact segRLs [h] [h', h] 1110 110 becomes segRLs [h] [h, h', h] 1110 110 (Rocq: seg_h1_ld).
  • Inc_spec (Inc(r,r') implies sideRLs [h] <r> <r'>). By induction on Inc, using the six machine-level h-pass rules of §2 for I1–I4.
  • Z1 and Z0. These use zero passes, so they hold by sideRLseq_O (via applys_eq) and the stream identities 1 100 0^inf = 110 0^inf and 1 000 0^inf = 100 0^inf (const_unfold).

3.2 Sweep (BigStep) #

Suppose R(hx,0,r,r'). The left part 0^inf 1 is never read by the pass, so:

S(r) = 0^inf 1 · 1 D> <r> -+-> 0^inf 1 <C <r'> -(3)-> 0^inf 11 D> <r'> = S(r')

The first arrow is the single pass h' (sideRLs_1); the second is the wall rule. This is TM7's BigStep, with the same proof. It compiles (§7).

3.3 The domain is closed (RIncs_nxt') #

Let P(r) := exists r', R(hx,0,r,r'). We need R(hx,0,r,r') to imply P(r').

Why the extension is needed. Without Z0/Z1 this is false:

  • r = LLLL10LL1L1 is in P, and its sweep is LLLLL1LLLLLLL111, which is not in P without Z0/Z1. Its second sweep needs Z1 and gives LLLLLLLLLLLLLL011L, which is also what the real machine produces.
  • Up to 11 digits, 170 words fail this way.

Z0/Z1 are never used on the run from blank (§7), so they matter only to the domain lemma.

Lemma A says one extra pass after the k passes is +1 on the result. It is what turns the second sweep's +1 passes into extra count for the first.

Key lemma (RIncs_nxt'). Two sweep processes run over the same digits:

  • the first has R(tp,k,r,r1);
  • the second reads the first's output after j pending +1s, R(tp,k+j,r,rK) (by Lemma A this is r1 + j), with count q.

Write K = k+j. The lemma:

R(tp,k,r,r1) /\ R(tp,K,r,rK) /\ Inv(tp,k,j,tq,q)  ->  exists r2, R(tq,q,rK,r2)
  Inv(hx,k,j,hx,q) := k <= q /\ j <= q
  Inv(h1,k,j,hx,q) := k+1 <= q /\ j <= q
  Inv(h1,k,j,h1,q) := (j = 0 /\ 2k+1 <= q) \/ (j >= 1 /\ K <= q /\ 1 <= q)      [both imply K <= q, 1 <= q]
  Inv(hx,_,_,h1,_) := False

With j = 0 these are exactly TM7's conditions (k<=k', k+1<=k', k*2+1<=k'). The domain step is the instance tp = tq = hx, k = j = q = 0.

Proof. Induct on the derivation of R(tp,k,r,r1), generalizing j, rK, tq and q. The induction is on the derivation, not on r, because X1/C1 recurse on inc r. In each case, invert R(tp,K,r,rK); Inc is functional, so both derivations name the same incremented word. The second process reads the first digit of rK, and that leaves one IH obligation. Each obligation's count K' is supplied by Lemma A, since the second's +1 passes land on the first's rest.

3.4 Progress #

R is inductive, so every derivation is finite. Each clause is a finite run: its segment lemma, plus the finitely many passes of its premises (§3.1). So S(r) -+-> S(r') is a finite run of at least 4 steps: h' takes at least 1, and the wall takes 3. Example: S(1) to S(L1) takes 11 steps (steps 5 to 16).

3.5 The halting transition F1 #

Formally, every rule is a proved exact run, so no step on the way from c0 is the halting transition.

As a sanity check: F is entered only by A1 = 0RF and E0 = 0RF. In the machine-level rules of §2, F is entered on the last cell of a digit, and that cell is 0 for all three digits, so F0 = 0RB fires. In every simulated rule instance F read a 0; the count of F reading 1 is 0 (§7).

5. Conclusion #

Use multistep_nonhalt with c' = S(1) (§4), then progress_nonhalt_cond with P(r) := exists r', R(hx,0,r,r'):

  • §3.2 gives S(r) -+-> S(r').
  • §3.3 gives P(r').
  • §3.4 shows each sweep is at least one step.

So the machine never halts. In Rocq this is TM7's nonhalt with RIncs replaced by R and with a different call to the domain lemma. TM7 calls RIncs_nxt with (tp':=hx) (k':=O) on one hypothesis. Here the call is RIncs_nxt' at tp = tq = hx and k = j = q = 0. Both R hypotheses are I1 (K = k+j = 0), and Inv(hx,0,0,hx,0) is discharged by lia.

In the .v file c' = S(L1) (see §4), RIncs_nxt' is named key, and the instance above is the lemma domain.
