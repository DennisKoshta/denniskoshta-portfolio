BB(6) strategy: where to put effort next

2026-10-14. This merges five strategy lenses: census, verified deciders, family proofs, equivalence twins and cryptids. Paths are relative to [local working file] Numbers marked "est." are estimates; every other number comes from the lens data (reports/strategy/census*.tsv, reports/strategy/equivalence-candidates.tsv).

The short version

How the list shrinks. The 740 holdouts are only 477 distinct tables. 70% of all kills since December came from multi-machine batch files, and 19% from decider certificates. Single-machine proofs move the list one line at a time.

Our position. Our own route (one machine or one table at a time, near a proof we already have) is running out of targets: - No free holdout sits within 1 transition of any of our 30 on-list kills. - Two of Wave 6's 8 picks parked as cryptid candidates on their first day.

The opportunity. The biggest swath nobody is closing fast is a single Skelet-1 core, K42. It accounts for 40-45 lines (5.4-6.1% of the list), all free. It is also a hard residue: upstream has already proved 16 other exit variants of the same core (two deciders plus hand proofs), and these 40 have not moved since April (see the Critic section).

Two near-free cuts come first: - our 18 kills that are checked but not submitted; - about 8 twin ports of proofs that already exist upstream.

Recommendation. After those, fund three things: 1. K42, which turns into a 40-line cryptid write-up if it fails. The critic lowered its odds; it is a residue of upstream methods. 2. A halting-certificate pipeline. Keep it short: someone else is already sweeping these pools. 3. Chaotic Bell Class 3 as a "cryptid or family" probe. Start with the write-1 exit question in the Critic section.

Stop decider parameter sweeps, and run no more one-off waves after Wave 6.

2. K42: one Skelet-1 core, 40-45 lines

The family. Every member is the same 5-state core 1RB0RC_1LC0RD_0LD0LC_1RE0LA_0RA0RF plus an escape state F (F1 is the only halt). Members differ only in: - F0 (16 (E1, F0) combinations in all: 9 F0 values with E1 = 0RF, 7 with E1 = 1RF); - whether E1 writes 0 or 1; - the start state, up to mirror.

Why it is attractive: - Size. 40 lines at 1 transition or less, on 16 tables (45 lines at 2 or less; the census component of 43 spans 17 tables). Not one is claimed by us, Astra or dyuan. - Shared grammar. At 1e8 steps all 43 census-component members show the same record shape: 0100^a 1^b 0001^c, or its mirror. - F is rare. It is first entered at 39,734-49,252 steps, then 0-6 more times up to 3e8. - The representation already exists upstream. At each F entry the tape is mxdys's digit representation from Sk1like_TC_v1.v. - Fit with our record. Our best class so far is Skelet 1-like Class 2 (12 of 14 killed, PRs #15-#18), and PR #18's closed-form Sk1Auto run reached 1.77e44 steps.

Why it may not work: - It is the residue of three upstream routes. Upstream proves 16 (E1, F0) exit variants of this exact core, not 6:

hand proofs (TCv3.v, Sk1like_TC_v1.v, Sk1like_TC_v2.v) 2
RWLAcc (RWLAcc_v2.v) 0 new (other starts of an hlin variant)

The 40 holdout lines are the other 16 variants. They have not moved since the 2026-04-26 list. The count was 69 in Aug 2025 and 46 in Feb 2026; the last 6 left between the Feb and Apr lists, when Sk1like_TC_v1/v2 landed (2026-03-11). Nothing has left since. - mxdys's note on line 496 says "~10^64 tape size and doesn't halt or become a TC yet". Running longer has already failed, so a kill needs an invariant. - The 40 variants split into 20 distinct F-entry histories. - One member (315) shows a residue branch toward halting.

Scoop risk: medium. The author of Sk1Machines.v works through a numbered "Sk1 Class1 TM1..TM105" list, but none of its 5 machines is on this core. mxdys's 8 Skelet 1-like Class 1 equivalence merges on 10-10 were outside it too: the core count was 40 on both the 778 and the 740 list.

First step (after the ports in section 1): an F-entry atlas, 6-8 h, Python only. Build an exact digit-level simulator, run the 20 distinct variant histories far past 3e8, and record the configuration at every F entry. Then answer three questions: - Do the F entries follow a recurrence in their parameters? - Can E ever read the 11 that leads to a halt? - Is there a digit-language invariant the core preserves, so each F0 only has to be checked once?

Some pairs already share near-identical histories (67 with 263; 64 with 124, offset by about 1.2k steps), which halves the per-variant work.

Why the atlas must be digit-level (critic). Sixteen members, 64 and 124 among them, enter F for the last time before 8e6 steps; lines 413 and 420 never enter it again after step 49,249. That looks like 64 and 124. But 3e8 steps is a tiny prefix: TM1's proof state alone is about 2e6 cells. So the step logs can only order the work. They cannot tell the members apart.

Gate. This lifts the "mined" exclusion on Skelet 1-like Class 1, so it needs Dennis's go. Ask in #bb6 first (message below).

Stop rule: - If the atlas finds no recurrence, or finds a pseudo-random digit map, apply the cryptid protocol: write the rules, flag it, stop. - If it is a go, write one core proof with Section + Variable premises (check_kill bans Hypothesis) plus generated per-variant modules. Stop if the first table takes more than 12 h without closing.

3. Halting-certificate pipeline

The idea. Find halters at the level of the abstract map, which is cheap with big integers. Then certify each one with a generated checked runner. Three of our kills were made this way: - C2-552 (PR #18); - W5-7; - W5-5 (1.82e18 steps in 751 chunks).

The family is the certifier, not an invariant.

Pools: - 136 lines with Collatz-type rules: Bell-like 63 of 77, Quadratic Mess 37 of 47, Cubic Mess 36 of 45. Rulefit has closed rule tables for 31 of them (11 + 8 + 12). - The Chaotic Bell Class 1 rate-2/3 cluster: 21 lines on 8 tables. W6-4 holds 6 of them. - mxdys's UBRRBA list: 74 lines still on the list. 20 are Astra's Bouncer + Chaotic Edge lines; coordinate on those.

Our halting hunt skipped the mined classes, so we have never swept this pool. Someone else almost certainly has (critic): - Cubic50.v, CubicMachines.v and CubicMachines_v2.v work through a numbered cubic.txt list (TM1 to TM50), on both the halting and the nonhalting side. - Bell60.v is a "complete RLE accelerator" with a halting certificate. - QuadraticMess30.v adapts lemmas "from the halting-proof archive supplied by the user".

So the Collatz-bell lines still listed are mostly the ones that author's RLE and affine-memo engine did not certify. Our edge is only map-level closed forms beyond RLE range. That is why the odds above are lowered to 0.2-0.25 for at least one halt.

Astra overlap. Astra is currently working on lines 257, 535 and 669 as nonhalting targets. These are one table of the Chaotic Bell Class 1 21-line cluster. Coordinate before touching that table.

Scoop risk is high. mxdys and the RLE-engine author posted Bell60.v, QuadraticMess30.v, Cubic50.v, CubicMachines_v2.v and ChaoticBell1Halt.v in a single week. Speed matters more than polish here: certify each halt and post it the same day.

First step, 8-12 h, Python only. Iterate the exact maps of the 31 closed-table lines, and run the 21 Chaotic Bell Class 1 lines at map level, coordinating with W6-4. The output is a list of predicted halters with step counts. Then: - Certifier generator: 8-12 h, adapted from W5-5's runner and the Sk1Auto / CBSim patterns. - Each halter: 2-6 h.

Stop rule: - If no line among the 31 + 21 reaches a halting branch within a certifiable range (PR #18 reached about 1e44 with closed-form batches), stop. Do not extend to the 105 lines without closed tables. - Optional extra: extract UBRRBAHaltv2.v's checker and rerun it on the 74 lines at 10x mxdys's budget. That is 6-10 h for p 0.2-0.3, but cheap budgets are probably exhausted (only 1 of 86 has fallen to it since 08-24).

5. Regular-language search (background, after Wave 6)

The idea. mxdys's CTL and FAR deciders only try automata from a few generator families. A complete SAT / meet-in-the-middle search over arbitrary automata, at 16-24 states per side, is a different test. It can run table by table over the 115 chaotic and Unknown tables. A weighted-FAR screen (Iijil's MITMWFAR) adds exact counter comparisons, which CTL cannot express.

What is already in place: - A native checker (ctlm) already replays upstream's CTLCertv1.v certificates. - Hits can be emitted in that file's format, so no new soundness proof is needed.

Before starting. Coordinate with Astra, whose primitive-free regular-certificate checker avoids the PArray/Uint63 problem with our check_kill gate. As of 14:32 today, Astra's standalone NFA certificate generator (astra/autonomous-20261014/certificate-package/package.py) passes the unchanged check_kill gate with zero extra assumptions on known sources. So a hit can be packaged without touching the baseline. Astra has offered to package any finite certificate we route to it.

Stop rule. One funded stage. If there is no hit on the 115 tables, stop. For calibration, today's probe of mxdys's own generators returned 0 hits in 473 runs on 40 lines.

Cryptids: yes, start two, but as families

What a cryptid deliverable is worth. A cryptid write-up does not shorten the list. It explains lines, and the community records that. The established form is: - a Rocq file of verified macro rules ("halts iff ..."); - a wiki entry.

Upstream has 11 such files (for example AntiHydra.v, HaltingCryptidCandidate1.v, and 1RB0LD_1LC0RA_1RA1LB_1LA1LE_1RF0LC_---0RE.v, "add rules for cryptid candidate").

Pick families, not single machines. Our one-off work already drifts into cryptids by itself: 14 of our machines (all on the 740 list) are now parked as cryptid candidates, 2 of them today. So the two cryptid starts should be families, where one write-up covers many lines and the same probe can still produce kills:

  1. K42 (40-45 lines). If the direction-2 atlas says "pseudo-random digit system", the deliverable is a rules file for the core plus a note covering all 40 lines. This is the largest unexplained family on the list.
  2. The Chaotic Bell Class 3 core (12 lines, 3 tables). There is no competition. The deliverable is a two-array rules file and a write-up, unless the epoch map turns out regular, in which case it becomes kills.

Reserve: Chaotic Bell Class 1 x3/2 (21 lines on 8 tables). It has the Hydra shape: a 3/2 map with a halting residue. Take it up only if W6-4 and direction 3 find no halts there.

Not worth agent-hours: - the famous cryptids (Antihydra, the BMO problems): these are open mathematics problems; - more work on our 13 parked candidates.

The one cheap exception is a rules-only file for Skelet 1-like Class 2's last two lines (R4 and #32), which would close that class's story as "12 killed + 2 formal cryptids". Do it only if Dennis wants it.
