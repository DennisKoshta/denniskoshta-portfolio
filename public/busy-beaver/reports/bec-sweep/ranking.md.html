Bell eats counter sweep: ranking (2026-10-13)

This ranks the untouched Bell eats counter machines on the 740 list for an attack now. The per-machine data is in reports/bec-sweep/universe.tsv.

Scout signals are evidence only. Only a tools/check_kill.sh PASS counts as a kill.

Corrected by the Critic section at the end: B-1 to B-3 run the dynamics of 693 (mxdys, "Chaotic"), so they now need Dennis's go. B-7 (707) is withdrawn because it was parked with W4-7. Only B-4 can be launched now.

In short

The class is thinner than its size suggests. Of the 21 untouched machines:

  • 6 are excluded as claimed by others;
  • 4 are exact trajectory twins of our own parked machines (identical tape at step 1e9);
  • 7 share a transition table with one of our parked machines, so they inherit its open lemma;
  • only 4 are genuinely fresh: 528, 310, 548 and 239.

No universe machine is within 4 transitions of any of our 9 BEC kills (the nearest is 5). So no pick is a cheap sibling or a one-transition port, as W4-1, W4-2 and W5-1 were.

Recommendation: launch B-1 and B-2 now (one mechanism, two machines). B-3 follows once B-1 has a round map, after Dennis says whether its link to 693 counts as a claim. B-4 is the bold pick. B-5 to B-8 restart families that we parked under a stop rule, so each needs Dennis's go and a new idea first.

Method

  • Simulation: C engine from the blank tape to 1e7, 1e8 and 1e9 (span, nonzeros, growth exponent). No machine halts by 1e9.
  • Records: tools/compress.py, the last 300 records before 1e8 and 1e9 (dominant skeleton, number of fields and bell blocks).
  • Distances: transitions changed, minimised over all state renamings (any start) and the mirror, against our 9 BEC kills (#2, #30, #7, W4-1, W4-2, W5-1, W5-3, W5-4, W5-5), all our other machines, and 462 upstream BEC/Bell/SOMDC proofs (data/solved_index.tsv plus BellEatsCounter_1RB0RF.v and ChaoticBell1Halt.v from origin/BB6).
  • Families: same table up to renaming and mirror, over the whole 740 list.
  • rulefit: tools/rulefit.py fit --budget 75 --top 2.
  • Twins: the 1e9 tapes compared exactly, and every record tape to 1e9 hashed (with its mirror) to look for merges at different step counts.
  • Halting: no machine halts by 1e9, and 239 runs to 2e10 without halting (its reservoir emptied twice on the way). Rulefit found no halt branch on any pick. The Wave 5 jump lens found no linear reservoir except 239's.

Finding 1: 528, 310 and 548 carry 693's bell values

The sweep's twin test hashed whole record tapes. That misses two kinds of twin:

  • the same field values written in other words (528 uses 5-cell words, 693 uses 4-cell words);
  • the same active part next to different fossil debris at the far end.

So I compared field values instead. At every left-edge record I read the two bell fields (c, d), meaning the runs of at least 40 words, and looked each pair up in 693's run to 1e9.

machine late-half left visits (c, d) pairs also in 693's run step ratio to 693 (last 50)
548 (B-3) 232 232 0.998
528 (B-1) 206 196 1.248
310 (B-2) 186 177 1.497

The step ratio is constant, so each machine is a time-rescaled copy of the same abstract dynamics. Taking all records instead of left visits gives the same answer: 97% of 528's late records carry a (c, d) pair from 693's run. For example:

528 reaches (c, d) = (307, 359) at step 995,707,720
693 reaches (c, d) = (307, 359) at step 797,816,274

The reset sequences match too. A reset is a left visit at which c falls. To 1e9, 528 has 71 resets and 310 has 65. Each sequence equals the start of 693's sequence, except one reset early on (49 units instead of 50, near c + d = 321). 548's 80 resets equal 693's exactly.

What this means:

  • By the sweep's own rule (693 is excluded because mxdys analysed it on the sheet), B-1 to B-3 inherit 693's status. Only Dennis can say whether a 2024 sheet analysis ("Results: Chaotic", last Discord link 2024-11-03) counts as a claim.
  • The risk is not "medium-high". These machines run exactly the dynamics that mxdys already judged chaotic.
  • A proof would cover four machines (528, 310, 548 and 693), with one abstract argument and separate low-level rules for each encoding.

The structure is not obviously chaotic. 693 has 80 resets to 1e9:

  • 69 follow a binary ruler: the cycle 1, 10, 4, X repeats, with X = 24·2^v − 2 (22, 46, 94, 190) and v following the ruler sequence 0, 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 0, 3.
  • 11 are irregular, in four bursts at c + d ≈ 91-153, 220-228, 321 and 475-537 (the last one moves 3, 59, 256, 431 and 8 units). The ruler restarts after each burst.

So this looks like a bell eating a binary counter, with the bursts as the "eat" events. The bursts are probably what mxdys called chaotic. If Dennis says go, the first hour should go to the bursts on 693's own run (the fastest encoding), under the cryptid stop rule.
