2.5 What the sweep adds

What was run (all on this box, about 15 minutes wall time on 12–16 cores; outputs are in the session scratchpad, see Appendix): - tools/sim/tmsim: every holdout to 1e9 steps, sampling the visited width every 1e7 steps. Then the same run on the 563 machines proof-killed since Dec. - tools/qsim.py (sligocki Quick_Sim, 20k loops): rules proven, steps reached, block size. - A neighbour search: every variant within 2 transition changes (about 38k per machine) was keyed by holdouts.equiv_key (mirror + state renaming) and looked up among the 235,762 proved 6x2 machines and among the other 777 holdouts.

Growth signatures are class-consistent: - Bell eats counter grows as t^0.31–0.36. - Bouncer + Chaotic Edge, Skelet 1-like Class 2, SO structural counter and Structural counter + chaotic edge grow as ~t^0.5. - Structural counter + counter grows as t^0.33. - Counter Class 3/4 barely grow (≤ 140 cells after 1e9 steps). - Translated Counter grows linearly.

That makes a cheap first check at Checkpoint A: a machine whose growth exponent disagrees with its sheet class is misclassified or special.

Trajectory twins: two holdouts count as twins when their visited width agrees to within 0.3% + 3 cells at every 1e7-step checkpoint up to 1e9 and their nonzero count at 1e9 agrees to within 0.5% + 3. - 47 twin groups were found, and every one lies within a single sheet class. - Validation: the detector flags 4 of mira-quine's 10 claimed equivalence pairs as exact twins, and 6 of 10 within 2%. - Twins that differ in 5–11 transitions are either non-obvious equivalences or the same mechanism under a different encoding. Either way, one proof idea serves the whole group.

Template neighbours: 95 of the 430 eligible holdouts are within 2 transitions of a machine with a hand-written proof (not a decider certificate). The pool uses the best of these.

What it does not tell us: a neighbour that is proved does not guarantee that its template transfers. For the one-transition variants, mxdys's own family search evidently ran and failed, so Checkpoint A has to name what the changed transition does.

4.1 How the pool was selected

The gate (applied to every representative and to every member of its equivalence group): 1. Index: holdouts.py solved verdict absent. There must be no row in data/solved_index.tsv in any tier (exact, halt notation, or equivalent up to mirror/renaming). Sheet tabs: not on the Level 1 (41) or Category 1 (16) tab. 4. Status: blank sheet Results status (excludes the 18 CRYPTIDs and the SOCSSv5 anomaly).

That leaves 430 eligible. Within the target classes of §2.3 they were ranked by: - (a) family leverage: trajectory twins, and open machines 1 transition apart; - (b) template distance: a hand-written proof 1–2 transitions away, preferably from 2026; - (c) class temperature: cooled or untouched beats hot; - (d) cost signals: slow growth, no 100 KB-class precedent.

Ranks 1–20 are leads, one per independent mechanism, spanning 11 classes; this is the recommended pilot. Ranks 21–40 are follow-ons (same mechanism as a lead, so cheap once the lead is proved) plus second-wave picks. If a lead dies at Checkpoint B, skip its follow-ons. R1–R11 are reserves.

Every machine in the pool and reserve is a singleton equivalence group, so each kill removes exactly one list entry.

Appendix: method notes and reproduction

  • Sweep commands. The direct simulation ran as tools/sim/tmsim '<tm>' --max-steps 1000000000 --every 10000000. Quick_Sim ran as .venv/bin/python -B tools/qsim.py '<tm>' --max-loops 20000 --max-seconds 45 --every 0 --no-tape. Both were driven by xargs -P 12 over data/holdouts/2026-10-07_778.txt. The 563 killed machines are the reason=proof rows of reports/forensics/kills.tsv with list windows from Dec 2025.
  • Where the outputs live. The scratch scripts (features, twin clustering, neighbour search, pool tables) are in the session scratchpad, not the repo, because this task scoped writes to this memo. Saving them as tools/analysis/targeting_*.py and the per-machine features as reports/targeting/*.tsv is a 10-minute follow-up if wanted.
  • Neighbour search. For each holdout, all 1- and 2-transition variants (25 options per transition: {0,1} × {L,R} × {A..F} or ---) were normalized with holdouts.equiv_key and looked up. Proved = proves ≠ - in solved_index.tsv. A neighbour counts as "hand-written" unless its file is a decider_certificate or an equivalence file.
  • Twin rule. Visited width (hi − lo + 1) agrees within 0.3% + 3 cells at all 101 samples (every 1e7 steps, 0 to 1e9), and nonzeros at 1e9 agree within 0.5% + 3. "Near-twin" in the tables means the maximum log-ratio over checkpoints 1e8..1e9 is below 0.02.
  • Behavioural look-alikes to killed machines (same metric) are weak evidence, since many bells and bouncers share growth constants. They are cited only below 3% and only as hints.
  • Caveats. Class labels come from the sheet's Classifications tab (Dec 2025), which has a few misfits. For example, R1 looks more like a sync bouncer counter than a fractal. "Hot", "cooling" and "untouched" come from commit dates and file headers, not from Discord.
  • Vendor hygiene. vendor/ was used read-only, and find vendor -name __pycache__ is empty.
