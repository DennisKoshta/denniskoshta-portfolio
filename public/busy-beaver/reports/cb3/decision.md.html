Question

The extra 1 written at the exit turned out to be bounded: - it merges into the stripe within two bounces; - it leaves at most 9 loose marks and at most 7 parity defects over 10^10 steps.

So the 12 lines should have a regular invariant. It just isn't one that upstream's sibling recipes find. Should we build our own closure checker for this core?

Answer

Dennis, 2026-10-14: A (build the token-level closure explorer; package any closed set through Astra's gate-passing certificate package).

Outcome of option A (stages 1-2)

The stop rule fired after about 5.75 agent-hours, so option A is stopped. There is no kill and no closed set: the explorer did not close even the calibration sibling S0LC, which FAR proves upstream. So nothing went to packaging or check_kill. T1 ran only in stage 1, and T2 and T3 were never explored.

Each stage stopped on the size trigger: - Stage 1: the cluster alphabet went past 40 words. Runs of separators, the period-4 stripe 0110 0110 ..., entered the alphabet one width at a time. - Stage 2: packs became tokens, but the cluster words went past 40 again, or a cluster went past 20 cells. The cause is that the abstract left side lets 5-segments repeat without limit, and a left sweep turns them into touching separators. The real orbit never has more than 2 in a row.

The other trigger never fired. No abstract halt appeared, and no closure needed a gap-length or parity condition, so the cryptid check stays negative.

The full log and the facts we learned are in explorer.md (section "Outcome"). Ledger rows are in coord/attempts.tsv; run python3 -B tools/attempts.py query CB3.
